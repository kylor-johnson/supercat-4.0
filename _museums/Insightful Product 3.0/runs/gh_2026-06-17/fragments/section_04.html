Investigative Context

Declining unit prices may reflect several dynamics: competitive pricing pressure driving buyers to request discounts, a natural shift toward lower-priced product tiers, changing buyer mix within an account, or seasonal promotional activity. These patterns warrant individual account review — they are signals to investigate, not conclusions to act on.

Action: The accounts worth a conversation first are the ones where volume is strong while unit prices slip — Wayfair ($608K current-quarter business on 776 orders) and Be Casual Furnishings ($470K, with a 16% price drop) suggest active, growing relationships shifting toward lower-priced product rather than churning. Liddon Furniture's 46% drop on just 11 orders is more likely a mix anomaly than a trend, so prioritize the high-volume accounts for a product-mix review.
Action: eCat already carries $50.2M of ordering with stable acquisition and a firming average order value, and $8.1M more runs through your own web and EDI — so the lever isn't a bigger eCat share, it's digitizing the $33.1M still keyed in by reps and the back office (often phone and email) and converting more of your 2,954 high-value quotes (3.3x the standard order). Validating the all-channel data feed would quantify how much of that rep-entered volume is phone/email today and confirm whether the unit-price softening across 20 accounts is mix shift or genuine pricing pressure.
