Behavioral Scorecard

Platform engagement data reveals how your top 20 reps by iPad GMV move through the selling funnel — from customer targeting to product discovery, configuration, presentation, and order submission over the trailing 12 months. Three patterns stand out:

Browse-Heavy, Low-Configure

Katrinka Barnhart leads the team in customer targeting activity (3,358 events) but converts to product discovery at just a 0.86:1 ratio — the lowest among the top 20. She is spending disproportionate effort selecting customers relative to browsing products, which limits the pipeline feeding her configuration and ordering workflow.

Configure-Heavy, Low-Present

Bobby Hansard configures 460 items but presents only 14 — a 3% pass-through rate from configuration to customer-facing sharing. Similarly, Jaime Hernandez (263 configurations, 0 presentations) and Jane Harrington (154 configurations, 0 presentations) skip the presentation step entirely. These reps build detailed orders but bypass the product-sharing workflow that drives larger, more informed purchases.

Present-Heavy, Low-Close

Brian McKinney delivers 147 customer-facing presentations but closes just 10 orders — a 7% conversion rate from presentation to submission. With the team's highest AOV ($8,309), even modest improvement in his close rate would produce outsized GMV impact. By contrast, Andrea Teague converts at 20:1 orders-to-presentations, closing efficiently with minimal formal presentation activity.

What this tells you: The widest funnel gaps on your team sit between configuration and presentation (Bobby Hansard, Jaime Hernandez, Jane Harrington) and between presentation and order closure (Brian McKinney, Jack Johnson). Closing these two gaps represents the highest-leverage coaching investment for mid-tier reps — the opportunity is not more activity, but better conversion of existing effort into orders.

Archetypes Are Coaching Tools, Not Labels

These archetypes describe how each rep naturally sells, not how well they sell. A Volume Relationship Seller needs AOV support, not breadth training they've already mastered. A Precision Closer needs ordering frequency incentives, not configuration coaching. Aligning coaching to style — rather than applying one-size-fits-all training — typically produces faster improvement.

What this tells you: Your team spans four distinct selling styles. Barbara Harper and Christie Schellenbach are Deep-Account Specialists — extracting maximum value from a tiny number of accounts, ideal candidates for adjacent-account expansion. Sharyn Moss is the quintessential Volume Relationship Seller — broadest reach at 46 customers but below-median AOV ($2,354), best supported with configuration and bundling strategies to lift order value rather than more customer visits.
