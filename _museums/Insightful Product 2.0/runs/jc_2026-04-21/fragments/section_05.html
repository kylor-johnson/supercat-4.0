eCat Ordering Paused Since January 2026

All 40 trailing-12-month iPad orders occurred in two bursts—3 in April 2025 and 37 in October–December 2025. Zero eCat orders have been submitted in the four months since (January–April 2026), while all-channel business has continued at a steady pace of 21–29 orders per month. This pattern is consistent with market-show-driven iPad ordering rather than continuous rep usage.

What this means: Jonathan Charles’s eCat ordering is event-driven, concentrated around what appear to be furniture market shows in Q4 2025. The $36,040 AOV reflects high-value luxury furniture transactions submitted in person during those events. The four-month gap since December 2025 is the most significant signal in this section—all-channel business remains active, but reps have not returned to the iPad ordering workflow since the last market cycle.

eCat captures 11.9% of all-channel order volume but 35.1% of all-channel GMV over the trailing 12 months. The 23-percentage-point gap means eCat orders are roughly 3× the value of the average all-channel order ($36,040 eCat AOV vs. $12,215 all-channel AOV). When reps use the iPad, they are placing the largest, most valuable transactions in the business.

What this means: eCat functions as a meaningful commerce channel for Jonathan Charles, capturing more than a third of total business value on just 12% of orders. The 3× AOV multiplier confirms the platform is the preferred tool for high-value market-show transactions. The dual-system dynamic is clear: routine orders flow through other channels, while the largest deals are placed on iPad. Reactivating eCat ordering in 2026 would recapture the high-value share that has gone dormant since December 2025.
