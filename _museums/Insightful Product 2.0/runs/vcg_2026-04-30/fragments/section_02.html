2.4 Coaching Opportunities

The following coaching opportunities are drawn from behavioral patterns in the scorecard and archetype analysis. Each card identifies a specific gap, a recommended action, and the potential impact of closing that gap.

Pete Gamber — Unlock Product Discovery
Discovery ratio of 0.7:1 is well below the 3:1 benchmark. With 2,615 customer touches but only 1,767 product browsing actions, Pete engages deeply with accounts but explores fewer products per visit than top-performing peers.
Action: Encourage broader product browsing during customer visits — using collections, filters, and related items to surface more of the catalog per interaction.
Potential impact: Matching the team’s top discovery ratio could expose Pete’s 22 active accounts to a wider assortment, potentially driving higher-value and multi-item orders.
Dawn Candee — Close the Discovery-to-Order Gap
Discovery ratio of 12.6:1 is the highest on the team, but only 6 orders from 69 presentations yields a close rate of 0.09 — the lowest among measured reps. The gap between product exploration and actual ordering is the widest on the team.
Action: Focus discovery sessions toward customer-specific selections and pair each catalog presentation with a structured ordering step to convert browsing into closed orders.
Potential impact: Raising her close rate even modestly — from 0.09 to the team’s median — could potentially double her order volume given her strong discovery foundation, adding an estimated $90K+ in incremental GMV.
John McLaughlin — Convert Showcasing Into Selling
Leads the team with 132 catalog presentations (PDF catalogs, email shares) but has placed only 6 orders — a 0.05 close rate. The app is being used primarily as a showcasing tool rather than a selling tool.
Action: Introduce a structured follow-up workflow after each catalog share — scheduling a review with the customer to convert catalog interest into placed orders.
Potential impact: Converting even 10% of his 132 presentations into orders could potentially generate $80K+ in incremental revenue based on his current AOV.
Dana Juneau — Add Presentation to the Mix
13 orders placed with zero catalog presentations — Dana goes directly from product discovery to ordering, bypassing the showcasing workflow entirely. While effective at closing, customers don’t receive pre-order catalogs or product information sheets.
Action: Add one catalog share per customer interaction — either a PDF before the sale or an email confirmation after — to reinforce product value and open the door for add-on items.
Potential impact: A presentation step could potentially increase per-order value by exposing customers to complementary products before finalizing, expanding deal sizes across her 11-account base.
Volume Sellers — Grow Average Order Value
Knaak, Fowler, Gamber, and Orange combine for 505 orders and $1.34M in GMV — but at average order values of $2,300–$2,850, they sit well below the org median of $8,059. Their order velocity is proven; the opportunity is in order value.
Action: Introduce curated product recommendations, premium collections, or add-on prompts to guide these reps toward higher-value assortments during existing customer interactions.
Potential impact: Each $1,000 increase in average order value across 505 annual orders could potentially represent roughly $500K in incremental iPad revenue — even a modest lift would be material.
