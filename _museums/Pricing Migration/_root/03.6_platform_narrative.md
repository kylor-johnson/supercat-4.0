## §1. Scope — what this document is, and what it is not

This document provides the narrative substance of the migration: the platform architecture story, the driver framings, and the health-evidence vocabulary. It governs every artifact type and every playbook — with one explicit carve-out:

### §1.1 Tailwind-exclusion clause (per chat B Stamp #2, 2026-05-27)

**Platform narrative framing per §§2–9 of this document does NOT apply to Good News Notice artifacts (Tailwind segment, 6 accounts — per `_root/01.5 §2`).**

The Good News Notice at `_root/03.5 §1.1` is deliberately short, operations-unchanged-voiced, and positive in register. Its purpose is to notify a price decrease clearly and close. Platform framing does not belong there for three reasons:

1. **Register mismatch.** The canonical paragraph (§2.1) and driver spines (§4) are architecture-forward; the Good News Notice is operations-unchanged-voiced per `_root/04 §4.5`. Weaving §2 content into a Tailwind notice pushes it out of register.
2. **Upsell drift risk.** The "What's Coming" section (§9) and platform-investment framing read as expansion signals. In a price-decrease notice, that reads as an upsell attempt and violates the "no upsell, no expansion ask" discipline at `_root/03.5 §1.1`.
3. **Unnecessary substance.** A Tailwind account is getting good news. Adding architecture narrative creates cognitive load with no benefit to the customer.

**Phase 4 template authors must NOT weave §2 canonical paragraphs, §3 principles, §4 driver spines, §5 health-band framing, or §9 "What's Coming" content into Good News Notice or Tailwind templates.** The Good News Notice structure is owned at `_root/03.5 §1.1`.

## §2. Canonical platform-positioning content

### §2.1 Canonical paragraph (verbatim — Source-fix Session F §I)

The following is the reusable block that appears (adapted for tone by health band per §2.2) across all non-Tailwind communications:

> SuperCat is moving all accounts to a standardized pricing architecture — three platform tiers based on the capabilities you use, with transparent, graduated user pricing. Your current terms reflect a legacy arrangement from [YEAR] that predates this structure. Every SuperCat account is migrating to the same architecture — consistent tiers, consistent user economics, consistent investment in the platform you depend on. What you get on the platform doesn't change — your catalog, your integrations, your rep access, your order workflows all stay exactly as they are.

**`[YEAR]`** is populated from the account's original agreement year per `_root/07` field guidance. Kylor and CEO should be able to deliver this paragraph from memory — it is the foundational story.

### §2.2 Health-band variants (verbatim — Source-fix Session F §I)

The canonical paragraph is the base. Append the appropriate variant based on the account's composite health score (`health_score_composite` per `_root/07`):

**Thriving variant (composite 90–100):** Append after the canonical paragraph:

> *"Your team is extracting exceptional value from the platform — [HEALTH_SCORE]/100 across team activity, feature usage, and business results. That investment is reflected in the tier you're on."*

**Healthy variant (composite 60–89):** Append:

> *"Your team has built a solid operation on SuperCat, and the platform is producing results across your business. The tier you're mapping to reflects the capabilities you're actively using."*

> *"We also want to make sure you're getting full value from the platform — [NAME] will follow up separately to review how your team is using SuperCat and whether there are opportunities we should address together."*

## §3. Communication principles (verbatim — Source-fix Session F §II)

Five rules that govern every piece of migration communication. These principles apply to artifact authoring, per-account conversations, and email drafting across all playbooks. Notice templates at `_root/03.5 §1` inherit these principles by reference — they are not restated in each template.

### Principle 1: Architecture-forward, never percentage-forward

Lead with what the account maps to and what it costs. Never lead with the percentage increase.

- **Say:** "Your account maps to Commerce Professional at $1,295/month."
- **Never say:** "Your price is increasing 43%."

The percentage is an internal metric. Externally, the customer should understand the new commercial structure and the value they receive. If the customer calculates the percentage themselves, acknowledge it — do not hide from it — but never introduce it.

### Principle 2: Lead with what the customer gets, not what SuperCat needs

The message is about the customer's tier, their included capabilities, and their usage — not about SuperCat's economics, cost structure, or revenue goals.

- **Say:** "Commerce Enterprise includes your full platform — iPad, catalog, portal, CPQ, credit card processing — with 40 included users."
- **Never say:** "We need to standardize pricing across the platform to support growth."

### Principle 3: Acknowledge legacy terms directly — never imply the customer was "getting away with something"

Many accounts have been below book pricing for years. That is commercially true but emotionally sensitive. The customer negotiated in good faith; they should not feel penalized for it.

- **Say:** "Your current rate reflects legacy terms from [YEAR] that we no longer offer. We're moving all customers to a consistent architecture."
- **Never say:** "You've been underpriced." / "You've been getting a deal." / "Your discount is being removed."
- **For accounts with explicit legacy discounts:** "Your account includes a [multi-org / platform / user rate] arrangement from your original contract. We're retiring all legacy arrangements simultaneously — you are not being singled out."

### Principle 4: Empathetic in tone, firm in architecture, flexible on mechanics

SuperCat can accommodate timing, billing date adjustments, user cleanup windows, annual prepay options, and multi-brand consolidation. SuperCat cannot recreate bespoke legacy pricing. Concession menu per playbook at `_root/04.5`.

- **Flexible:** "We can adjust the effective date to align with your budget cycle." / "If you clean up inactive users before the effective date, your user charges will reflect that."
- **Firm:** "We're not able to extend the legacy rate structure beyond the transition period."

### Principle 5: Artifacts over meetings — the document carries the substance

With a 2-person CS team, bespoke communication artifacts are the primary vehicle for substance delivery. A data-rich value summary delivered alongside the notice communicates more substance than a 20-minute call — and scales.

Meetings are reserved for: Entity parents (meeting proposed after packet delivery per `_root/01.5 §7`); Pre-Engagement accounts (CEO-initiated call before notice per `_root/01.5 §6`); inbound requests from any account. For Core + Narrative (28 standalone accounts), the digital artifact IS the personal outreach — no default meeting.

### §4.1 `user_rate_normalization` (37 accounts, +$14,383 delta)

**Messaging posture:** "User pricing standardized"

**Key framing:** Graduated rate vs. legacy flat; volume benefit

**One-sentence:** "User pricing is being standardized on a graduated schedule — $25 per user for the first 10 excess users, stepping down to $18 at higher volumes."

**Supporting evidence to include:**
- Current user rate vs. new graduated schedule
- Number of trailing average users and active users
- How many users are absorbed by the new included-user base (10, 15, or 40 depending on tier)
- Net user charge change (often offset by more included users)

**Tone:** Normalization framing. Emphasize that the graduated schedule rewards higher volume.

### §4.2 `platform_discount_correction` (17 accounts, +$8,817 delta)

**Messaging posture:** "Legacy discount retired"

**Key framing:** Acknowledge discount existed (per `discount_drivers` field); install-base normalization (internal framing — see §8 for client-facing language equivalent)

**One-sentence:** "Your platform base fee reflects a legacy arrangement from [YEAR]. All accounts are moving to the standard tier rate for the capabilities they use."

**Supporting evidence to include:**
- Legacy platform fee vs. standard tier base
- What the tier includes (feature inventory)
- Tenure on platform
- Discount driver detail from `discount_drivers` field

**Tone:** Direct acknowledgment — "your current rate reflects legacy terms" — not evasion. For deltas >$300/mo: *"You negotiated a discount at signing. We're retiring all legacy arrangements simultaneously. You are not being singled out."*

**Supporting evidence to include:**

**Key framing:** What's included in tier; excess priced transparently

**Supporting evidence to include:**

- How many users become excess and at what rate
- Offset from any base fee change

## §5. Axis 2 — health-profile framing

The health-profile fields (per `_root/07` field guide) determine the "here's the value you're receiving" substance. Health data is not supplementary — it is half the artifact for Executive and Pre-Engagement accounts, and a meaningful structural component of Simplified value summaries for Core and Narrative accounts.

### §5.1 Composite score + health bands (operator-stamped Session E Stamp #1)

The composite score (0–100, `health_score_composite` per `_root/07`) drives the headline positioning and overall artifact register:

| Band | Range | Headline positioning |
|---|---|---|
| **Thriving** | 90–100 | "Your team is extracting exceptional value — [SCORE]/100 across all dimensions." |
| **Healthy** | 60–89 | "Your operation on SuperCat is producing solid results." |
| **Watch** | 40–59 | Do not lead with value claim. Acknowledge relationship; offer to address product fit. |
| **At Risk** | 20–39 | As Watch; Strategic-segment routing per `_root/02 §4`. |
| **Critical** | 0–19 | As Watch/At Risk; Strategic-segment routing per `_root/02 §4` per-account judgment. |

Watch / At Risk / Critical accounts route to the Strategic playbook per `_root/02 §4` health-override triggers. This doc's §5.3 Watch+AtRisk band guidance applies to Strategic playbook artifact framing.

**Additional Axis 2 inputs beyond the composite score:**
- **Composite narrative** — direct quote from health system; use as personalized evidence in artifacts
- **Dimension narratives** — specific, actionable language about what's working in each dimension; use verbatim where available

### §5.2 Four-dimension model (operator-stamped Session E Stamp #1)

Four health dimensions generate targeted substance for the artifact's health-evidence section. Lead with the strongest dimension; acknowledge any Developing dimension as a joint opportunity:

| Dimension | Field (per `_root/07`) | High score → cite | Low score → frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Team activity** | `team_activity_score` | Login volume, rep penetration, pace trend | "We want to make sure your team is getting full value — [NAME] will follow up on rep activation." |
| **Feature usage** | `feature_usage_score` | Feature breadth, capabilities actively used | "There are capabilities at your tier you haven't activated yet — worth exploring together." |
| **Business results** | `business_results_score` | Active channels producing outcomes; ROI evidence | "Your catalog is set up; activating buyer-facing ordering would let buyers transact directly." |
| **System health** | `system_health_score` | Catalog completeness %, feed sync status, specific data facts | "Your import feeds are syncing on schedule and your catalog is [X]% complete." |

**Evidence writing rules (Source-fix Session F §IX):**
- Column header in the artifact: **"Details"** — not "What this means"
- Every evidence sentence must contain at least one concrete number or named feature the client recognizes
- Never use a term the client would need defined
- Use the client's language: reps, buyers, catalog, orders, iPad, cart — not "configured channels," "tracked outcomes," "value capture," "operational," "adoption"

### §5.3 Per-band tone modulation (integrated: Session E Stamp #1 + Source-fix Session F §IV)

#### Thriving (composite 90–100)

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Voice** | Confident, investment-forward |
| **Evidence emphasis** | Lead with team activity and feature usage stats as proof of platform centrality. Use phrases like "exceptional value," "platform investment," "core commerce infrastructure." |
| **Health badge** | Prominent, green-highlighted |
| **Delta framing** | Investment alignment — the customer is already getting outsized value; the pricing reflects that |

#### Healthy (composite 60–89)

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Voice** | Balanced, concrete, evidence-forward |
| **Evidence emphasis** | Lead with specific operational data (orders processed, catalog completeness, feed health). Avoid "exceptional" language — use "solid," "producing results," "actively using." |
| **Health badge** | Prominent, standard |
| **Delta framing** | Standardization focus — what the tier includes and what the customer is using. Heavier on concrete evidence, lighter on "investment" framing than Thriving. |

| **Follow-up timing** | Day 7 non-response |

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|

| **Concession posture** | More latitude — transition credits, extended timelines, bounded transition pricing. Per `_root/04.5` Strategic playbook concession menu and `_root/01.5 §8` walk-away-threshold framework. |

### §5.4 Support-fire framing (operator-stamped Session E Stamp #1)

**If an account has active support tickets or unresolved system issues at the time of migration communication:** acknowledge and address the issue BEFORE pairing the pricing notice. Do not deliver pricing while the customer's experience is actively degraded.

Framing: *"Before we walk through the migration, I want to make sure [issue] is fully resolved. [NAME] is following up on [ticket / issue description] — once that's addressed, we'll walk through the pricing update together."*

This rule applies across all health bands but is highest-urgency for Watch / At Risk accounts, where open support issues compounded with a pricing migration simultaneously threatens the relationship. Support-fire resolution is a sequencing gate, not an optional courtesy.

## §6. Axis 1 × Axis 2 composition rules (operator-stamped Session E Stamp #1)

**The artifact is NOT a template selection — it is a composition.** Every artifact is unique because the two axes produce different content for every account.

**Composition principle:**
- **Axis 1** (`migration_driver`) determines the pricing explanation content — what changed in the pricing and why (§4 driver framing)
- **Axis 2** (`health_profile`) determines the value demonstration substance and overall artifact register — what the account has built on SuperCat and how to frame the investment (§5 health-band + dimension data)

The two axes compose additively: Axis 1 provides the "why your price is changing" section; Axis 2 provides the "here's the value you're receiving" section and tunes the overall register of both. Neither axis alone produces a complete artifact.

**Composition sequencing within the artifact:**

1. Open with the canonical paragraph (§2.1) or the appropriate §2.3 register-tuned variant, with §2.2 health-band append
2. Driver explanation (§4 one-sentence + supporting evidence for this account's `migration_driver`)
3. Health evidence section (§5.2 four-dimension substance, ordered by dimension score high → low; composite narrative as opening evidence quote)
4. "What's Coming" section (§9) — in value artifacts and entity packets only; never in the formal notice
5. Options and next steps (concession menu per playbook at `_root/04.5`; contact information)

**Compound-driver accounts:** If the account has a secondary driver (per `_root/05 §4` weaving matrix), the secondary driver evidence appears in the artifact supporting section, not in the notice body. The notice names the primary driver only.

**Health-band override on register:** The health band modulates the overall register even when the driver framing is strong. A `platform_discount_correction` account with Watch health (Hudson Valley example — see §7) does NOT lead with discount-correction framing as the opening register — it leads with relationship acknowledgment and product-fit offer. The driver explanation is present but its position in the artifact shifts based on health band.

### §8.1 Internal-vs-external split rule

This document and other internal `_root/` substance docs use **"install-base"** (hyphenated, compound noun) as shorthand for an account's specific platform footprint — the modules, brands, users, and capabilities actively operating on SuperCat. This is the internal `_root/` convention.

**Client-facing artifacts and notices must NOT use "install-base" or "install base."** Session F §III row 11 puts both forms on the never-say list for client copy. The client-facing equivalents are:

| Internal `_root/` convention | Client-facing equivalent (Session F §III row 11 + §I language) |
|---|---|
| "install-base normalization" | "your subscription maps to the capabilities you're already using" |
| "normalized to its current install-base" | "normalized to the platform you depend on" |
| "the install-base you operate today" | "your catalog, your integrations, your rep access, your order workflows" |
| "install-base footprint" | "every SuperCat account" / "the platform capabilities you use" |

Phase 3c agents rewriting `_root/05 §2.X` driver blocks, Phase 4 template authors, and Phase 7 per-account drafters **must use the client-facing equivalents** listed above. The §2.3 Variant C register-tuned template uses parameterized slots that must be populated with client-facing equivalents when instantiated in a live artifact.

### §8.2 The `platform_discount_correction` driver and install-base normalization

The Axis 1 key framing for `platform_discount_correction` (§4.2) uses "install-base normalization" as the internal framing concept. In client-facing form, this becomes:

> "Your platform base fee reflects a legacy arrangement from [YEAR]. All accounts are moving to the standard tier rate for the capabilities they use."

The install-base-normalization concept is implicit in "the capabilities they use" — it is not named explicitly in client copy. The internal framing helps drafters and Phase 4 template authors understand WHY the framing is "standard tier rate for the capabilities they use" — because the transition normalizes the subscription to the actual platform footprint, not to a negotiated legacy rate. That explanation lives here; it never surfaces in client artifacts as the word "install-base."
