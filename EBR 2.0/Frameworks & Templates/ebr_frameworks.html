Executive Business Review — Template Frameworks

Three structurally distinct approaches, universal across all SuperCat clients

Working Draft · February 2026 · SuperCat Solutions

How to Use This Document

Three structurally distinct EBR frameworks are presented below. Each is designed to be universal across all SuperCat clients — ordering and non-ordering use cases, all segments, all module configurations. The frameworks differ in what leads the conversation and how data is positioned relative to strategic dialogue.

Each section is broken into five layers:

Purpose — why it exists
Content — what goes in it
Data Inputs — what populates it
Presenter Notes — how to deliver it
Template A

Discovery-Led

Open with the client's world, use data as supporting evidence mid-conversation, close with co-created next steps. The meeting is a strategic conversation that happens to be informed by data.

Best for: Relationship-first engagements where strategic dialogue and trust-building are the primary value. Clients who value being heard over being shown.

1

Partnership Snapshot

Purpose

Ground the room in the relationship. Not a company overview — a reminder that this meeting exists because of shared history and shared goals.

Content
  • Relationship tenure (e.g., "Partner since March 2023")
  • Active products — which of the four modules are live
  • Key stakeholders in the room and their roles
  • One-line framing: "This is your review — our goal is to understand where your business is heading and how we support that."
Data Inputs
  • Contract start date (HubSpot / Stripe)
  • Active module list (Stripe subscription data, Admin Console)
  • Contact roles (HubSpot contacts)
Presenter Notes

Spend no more than 90 seconds. This slide orients, not impresses. If ongoing relationship, reference what was agreed last session.

2

Your Business & Market

Purpose

Open the discovery conversation. Before any data is presented, understand what's changed in the client's world. This section is designed to listen, not present.

Content

3–5 pre-seeded questions tailored to the account:

  • "What's shifted in your market since we last spoke?"
  • "Are you expanding into new channels — hospitality, designers, new retail segments?"
  • "How is your sales team structure evolving? Territory changes or new hires?"
  • "Any changes on the ERP, supply chain, or fulfillment side?"
  • "What's keeping your leadership up at night right now?"
Data Inputs
  • Pre-meeting research: company news, trade shows, hiring signals, industry trends
  • Fathom call history: pain themes, competitor mentions, objections
  • HubSpot: open deals, recent engagement notes
  • Internal account brief: known business context, strategic opportunities
Presenter Notes

This is the most important section of the meeting. Resist the urge to fill silence. Questions should feel specific to this client — "I noticed you were at High Point Market last month — how did that go?" beats generic prompts. Capture responses; they inform everything that follows.

Use-Case Flex
Ordering clientsInclude questions about transaction workflows, channel mix preferences, order accuracy goals.
Non-ordering clientsLean into catalog presentation workflows, showroom strategy, buyer engagement patterns.
3

Platform Utilization Overview

Purpose

Objective snapshot of how the client's organization uses SuperCat today. Framed as operational visibility, not a scorecard.

Content
  • Module-level view: which products are active, user counts per module
  • Utilization rate: active users vs. licensed users
  • Engagement trend: MAU trend (6-month) showing trajectory
  • Feature adoption summary: capability clusters in use (Activity Ladder levels in business language)
  • Channel mix (if applicable): iPad vs. web usage patterns
Data Inputs
  • Active User Ratio (PostgreSQL MCP, Admin Console)
  • MAU Trend — 6-month (Mixpanel)
  • Feature Adoption Rate (Mixpanel, PostgreSQL MCP)
  • Mobile vs. Web Split (Mixpanel)
  • User Growth Trend (PostgreSQL MCP)
  • Subscription Completeness (Stripe, Admin Console)
  • Channel Revenue Share / Channel Adoption Trend — ordering clients only
Presenter Notes

Translate every metric into business language. Not "Feature Adoption Rate: 62%" but "Your team is actively using about two-thirds of the capabilities available — let's look at where the gaps are." 3–5 minutes of presentation with natural pauses for client reaction.

Use-Case Flex
Ordering clientsInclude order velocity (orders/active user, AOV, daily order avg) and channel performance (iPad vs. web revenue share, channel AOV).
Non-ordering clientsFocus on catalog engagement: PDF creation rate, product searches, list creation, SmartPicks, resource library. Frame as "how your team uses eCat as a selling tool."
4

Your Sales Team in Action

Purpose

Deliver rep behavioral intelligence the client wants for their own sales management. This is their data — SuperCat is the lens.

Content
  • Rep-level activity summary — door reports/SmartStack, placement views, SmartPicks, list creation, customer favorites/backorders, PDF catalogs, item emails, barcode scanning
  • Power user identification — who's using the platform deeply
  • Enablement opportunities — reps with low engagement on key behaviors
  • Activity Ladder summary — behavioral maturity framing (e.g., "Strong in product discovery, haven't yet adopted personalization tools")
  • Raw data export — CSV companion for stakeholders who want to slice it themselves
Data Inputs
  • All Activity Ladder events per rep (Mixpanel)
  • Feature Breadth Score per rep (Mixpanel)
  • User Concentration Risk (Mixpanel)
  • Rep Activity Ranking (Mixpanel)
  • PDF Catalogs, SmartPicks, Email Item Info, Scan Item per rep (Mixpanel)
  • Orders per Rep, Revenue per Rep (Admin Console) — ordering clients only
Presenter Notes

Frame as "here's what your team is doing" not "here's how your team scores." For non-ordering clients, explicitly acknowledge orders flow through other channels — show selling behaviors inside the platform. Have the raw CSV ready before the meeting. The correlation question will arise — invite clients to overlay their own performance data.

Use-Case Flex
Ordering clientsPair behavioral data with order metrics (orders/rep, revenue/rep, orders/login). Correlation story is self-contained.
Non-ordering clientsBehavioral data only. "Here's the engagement picture. You hold the sales data — let's talk about how these map to what you're seeing."
5

Your Customer Signals

Purpose

Surface intelligence about the client's own customer base — their retailers, dealers, distributors, designers. Revenue leak detection that only exists because operations flow through SuperCat.

Content
  • Inactive buyer flags — customers who didn't order this month when patterns suggest they should have
  • Reorder gap analysis — products historically purchased but not reordered in 6–8 weeks
  • Customer Activation Rate — % of customer accounts actively engaging
  • Self-Service Adoption (eCat Online clients) — which customers use self-service vs. fully rep-dependent
  • Top customer summary — most active, emerging, and declining accounts
Data Inputs
  • Customer Activation Rate (Admin Console, PostgreSQL MCP)
  • Digital Self-Service Rate (Mixpanel, Admin Console)
  • Order Frequency by customer (Admin Sales Summary)
  • Customer-level order history for gap analysis (Admin Sales Summary, PostgreSQL MCP)
  • Sales data pushed into eCat by the client (for non-ordering clients)
Presenter Notes

High perceived value — directly actionable in the client's next sales meeting. Position as "patterns the platform sees across your entire dealer network that individual reps might miss."

Use-Case Flex
Ordering clientsFull transaction-based analysis — order gaps, reorder patterns, customer AOV trends.
Non-ordering (sales data pushed)Same analysis using pushed sales data. Acknowledge data source difference transparently.
6

Opportunities on the Horizon

Purpose

Based on discovery and data findings, surface relevant opportunities. Should feel co-created, not prescribed.

Content
  • 2–3 specific opportunities tied to the conversation and data
  • For each: what it is, why it's relevant now, what it looks like in practice (anonymized peer context), next steps
  • Expansion signals from the metrics framework — only those relevant to this client
Data Inputs
  • Expansion Signals table (cross-referenced against client)
  • Subscription Completeness (Stripe, Admin Console)
  • Cross-Entity Feature Gap (Mixpanel) — multi-entity accounts
  • Industry Segment Benchmarks
  • Fathom pain themes
Presenter Notes

Never present more than 3 opportunities. Every one must connect to something the client said or the data revealed that the client confirmed matters. Peer context is anonymized — "Companies in your segment with similar catalog sizes typically see X."

7

Shared Priorities & Next Steps

Purpose

Close with a co-created action plan. The meeting produces commitments, not just conversation.

Content
  • 3–5 prioritized action items with owners (both sides) and target dates
  • Agreed cadence for next check-in
  • Data/deliverables to share post-meeting (CSV exports, follow-up analysis)
Presenter Notes

Write collaboratively in real time. Send formalized version within 48 hours. Feed all commitments back into the internal account brief and CRM.

Template B

Intelligence-Led

Lead with the data the client can't get anywhere else — rep behavior and customer signals — then broaden into strategic conversation. The intelligence earns the right to have the strategic dialogue.

Best for: Operationally-minded clients whose stakeholders are sales leaders and ops people. Clients who want to see the data first and discuss implications second. The BCG/Visual Comfort profile.

1

Partnership Context

Purpose

Brief anchor. Orient the room, establish the relationship frame.

Content

Same as Template A1. One-line framing: "Today we're sharing intelligence about your sales team and customer base, and we want your guidance on what matters most."

Presenter Notes

60–90 seconds. Move quickly — this audience wants the data.

2

Sales Team Performance Intelligence

Purpose

The headline section. Rep behavioral data the client needs for sales management — immediately actionable format.

Content
  • Rep Activity Dashboard — every rep mapped against key behaviors: door reports (by rep AND by customer), placements, SmartPicks, list creation/sharing, customer favorites/backorders, PDF catalogs, item emails, scanning
  • Power User Profiles — top 3–5 reps with specific behaviors highlighted
  • Enablement Gaps — reps with low/zero activity on key behaviors
  • Concentration Risk — disproportionate reliance on single rep flagged
  • Behavioral Trends — MoM comparison: team using platform more or less?
  • Raw Data Companion — full CSV of activity dataset
Data Inputs
  • Full Activity Ladder events per rep (Mixpanel)
  • All rep-level metrics from metrics framework Section 6
  • User Concentration Risk, Feature Breadth Score per rep (Mixpanel)
  • Login Frequency per rep (Mixpanel, Admin Console)
  • Orders/Rep, Revenue/Rep (Admin Console) — ordering clients only
Presenter Notes

This is the Beth Miller section. Lead with names, specific behaviors, specific numbers. Organize by rep, not feature — the client thinks in people. Have the CSV ready before the meeting starts. For non-ordering clients: "We're showing everything we can see about how your reps work inside the platform. The final transaction is where we'd love your input on correlation."

Use-Case Flex
Ordering clientsFull picture: behavioral + order data per rep. Orders/login, revenue/rep, channel per rep.
Non-ordering clientsBehavioral only. Include "correlation opportunity" note for client to overlay their performance data.
3

Customer Base Health

Purpose

Intelligence about the client's customers — retailers, dealers, buyers they serve.

Content
  • Inactive Buyer Flags — no order this month, historical patterns suggest otherwise
  • Reorder Gap Analysis — products historically purchased but not reordered in 6–8 weeks, as an actionable list
  • Customer Activation Rate — % of customer accounts actively engaging
  • Customer-Level Activity — for key accounts: which reps servicing, what activities happening
  • Self-Service Adoption (eCat Online) — which customers self-serve vs. fully rep-dependent
Data Inputs
  • Customer Activation Rate (Admin Console, PostgreSQL MCP)
  • Customer-level order/engagement data (Admin Sales Summary, PostgreSQL MCP)
  • Digital Self-Service Rate (Mixpanel, Admin Console)
  • Rep-to-customer activity mapping (Mixpanel — Select Customer, Search Customer events)
Presenter Notes

Inactive buyer list and reorder gap analysis are the highest-value deliverables. Present customer data by territory or rep assignment — that's how sales leadership thinks. Be transparent about data availability for non-ordering clients.

4

Platform & Data Health

Purpose

Is the engine running clean? Trust builder for admin/ops stakeholders.

Content
  • Data freshness: last product/customer/inventory update
  • Import success rate (30-day), integration status (FTP/API health)
  • Catalog completeness: products with images %, category/collection depth
  • Territory configuration status, error patterns
Presenter Notes

Keep brief — 2–3 minutes. If issues exist, surface proactively. If healthy, say so and move on. Universal across all clients.

5

Business Context & Market Check-In

Purpose

Discovery — but positioned after the intelligence. Both sides now have shared context to anchor conversation.

Content
  • Discussion anchored to data: "We saw [pattern] in your customer base — is that reflecting something in the market?"
  • "Your team's usage of [feature] increased — what's driving that?"
  • Open questions about business direction, competitive landscape, strategic priorities
Presenter Notes

More traction than Template A's discovery because both sides have shared context. Reference data conversationally — "We showed SmartPicks usage is low — is that a training issue, or does the feature not fit your workflow?"

6

Utilization & Growth Opportunities

Purpose

Module adoption view plus specific opportunities surfaced by the data. Natural conclusion to the intelligence story.

Content
  • Current module footprint vs. full platform capability
  • Unused capabilities tied to earlier findings
  • Peer context: what similar companies in their segment leverage
  • 2–3 prioritized recommendations with business case framing
Presenter Notes

Should feel like a natural conclusion, not a sales pitch. Every recommendation connects to something surfaced earlier.

7

Action Plan

Purpose

Prioritized next steps with owners and dates.

Presenter Notes

For this audience, be especially crisp and specific — ops-minded stakeholders want clarity, not open-ended commitments.

Template C

Benchmark-Forward

Structure the entire meeting around where the client sits relative to their potential — in platform utilization and business outcomes. Peer comparison and maturity framing drive the conversation.

Best for: Competitive, benchmark-oriented clients motivated by peer comparison and maturity frameworks. Clients who want to know where they stand.

1

Partnership Snapshot

Content

Same brief anchor as Templates A and B. 60–90 seconds.

2

Where You Stand: Adoption Maturity

Purpose

The centerpiece. A clear visual of where the client sits relative to the full platform capability set and relative to their peers.

Content
  • Module Adoption Map — visual of active vs. available products (green/gray)
  • Feature Utilization Heatmap — capabilities used deeply, lightly, or not at all, organized by Activity Ladder levels in business language
  • Peer Benchmark Overlay — anonymized comparison to similar companies by vertical/size/suite: "Companies like yours in lighting typically leverage these capabilities at these levels"
  • Maturity Stage — Early (foundational), Growing (expanding), Optimized (deep utilization). Non-judgmental, aspirational framing.
Data Inputs
  • Subscription Completeness (Stripe, Admin Console)
  • Feature Adoption Rate (Mixpanel, PostgreSQL MCP)
  • All Activity Ladder metrics (Mixpanel)
  • Industry Segment Benchmarks (129 active orgs, 5 verticals)
  • Benchmarking Dimensions: Org Size, Tenure, Product Suite, Integration Maturity
  • Active User Ratio, MAU Trend (PostgreSQL MCP, Mixpanel)
Presenter Notes

Needs strong visuals — client should grasp where they stand in 10 seconds. Peer benchmarks always anonymized. Frame gaps as opportunities: "You're strong in product discovery — companies that also activate personalization tend to see deeper engagement." This is where Kent Phillips lives.

Use-Case Flex
Ordering clientsBenchmarks include transaction metrics (AOV, orders/user, activation rate) alongside behavioral.
Non-ordering clientsBenchmarks focus on engagement depth, feature breadth, catalog utilization, selling behaviors. Exclude order comparisons or note "among clients who transact through eCat."
3

Your Reps vs. the Benchmark

Purpose

Apply benchmarking to the rep level — where do this client's reps sit relative to the 7,000+ rep universe?

Content
  • Activity Ladder Results — org aggregate at each level (Customer Targeting → Engagement Depth)
  • Segment Benchmark — this org's ladder profile vs. vertical peers
  • Rep Distribution — are a few power users pulling the average, or is adoption broad?
  • Power User Spotlight — top performers and what they do differently
  • Enablement Opportunities — low-adoption behaviors that peers have activated successfully
Data Inputs
  • Activity Ladder scoring per rep (Mixpanel, Admin Console User Feature Report)
  • Feature Breadth Score, User Concentration Risk (Mixpanel)
  • Segment-level Activity Ladder benchmarks (aggregated from 129 active orgs)
Presenter Notes

The 7,000+ rep benchmark is your unique asset — lean into it. Present org aggregate first, allow drill-down. Have CSV ready. Frame around potential: "Your team is strong at Levels 1–2 — the next unlock is typically Level 3–4 behaviors."

4

Your Customer Engagement Profile

Purpose

Customer intelligence with benchmark context.

Content
  • Customer Activation Rate vs. Segment Median
  • Self-Service Adoption benchmarked (eCat Online clients)
  • Inactive Buyer Flags and Reorder Gap Analysis
  • Top/Declining Customer Summary
Presenter Notes

"Your customer activation rate is 45% — segment median for furniture companies your size is 62%. Let's talk about what's driving that gap." Benchmark provides strategic context; inactive buyer lists provide tactical follow-up.

5

What's Changed Since Last Review

Purpose

Brief retrospective. Benchmarks and current-state carry the weight — this section provides continuity only.

Content
  • Progress against previously agreed priorities
  • Notable usage shifts (positive or negative)
  • Support themes worth highlighting
  • Milestones or achievements to acknowledge
Presenter Notes

Maximum 2–3 minutes. If prior actions weren't completed (by either side), acknowledge honestly.

6

Discovery: What's Ahead

Purpose

Strategic conversation — positioned after benchmarking so the client has context for what "more" looks like.

Content
  • "Based on where you sit relative to peers, what resonates? What surprised you?"
  • "Which gaps matter to you, and which don't?"
  • "Where are you headed in the next 6–12 months?"
Presenter Notes

The benchmarks seed the conversation. Listen for which gaps the client cares about vs. dismisses — this focuses expansion energy.

7

Recommendations & Roadmap

Content
  • 2–3 recommendations, each with: The Gap (benchmark finding), The Opportunity (business outcome), The Path (practical steps), Peer Evidence (anonymized results)
  • Rough timeline and ownership model
Presenter Notes

Only recommend closing gaps the client validated as important in discovery. Peer evidence is the unique asset — anonymized but specific.

8

Commitments & Next Steps

Content

Same as Templates A and B. For benchmark-oriented clients, commit to sharing updated benchmarks at next review — creates natural cadence.

How They Differ

Template A

Discovery-Led

Optimized for strategic relationship depth. The data serves the dialogue. Trust is the primary currency.

Data position: supporting evidence mid-conversation

Leads with listening

Template B

Intelligence-Led

Optimized for operationally-minded clients. Actionable intelligence delivered first, implications discussed second.

Data position: headline, front-loaded

Leads with data

Benchmark-Forward

Optimized for competitive clients. Peer comparison and maturity framing motivate deeper adoption.

Data position: contextualized by peer comparison

Leads with context

Cross-Template: Use-Case Profile Flag

All three templates require a use-case profile determination before meeting prep begins. This drives which metrics appear and how sections are framed.

Profile Characteristics Metrics Emphasis
Ordering Client Places orders through eCat iPad and/or eCat Online Full transaction + behavioral metrics
Non-Ordering / Sales Enablement Uses eCat for catalog, presentation, buyer engagement; orders via EDI/fax/email Behavioral and engagement metrics only; correlation opportunity with client's own data
Non-Ordering + Sales Data Pushed Same as above, but client pushes sales/invoice data into eCat Behavioral metrics + customer intelligence from pushed data
