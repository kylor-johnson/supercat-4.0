## A measurement correction that affects every count below

The Phase 2 feature-reach numbers came from a prebuilt BigQuery view
(`org_feature_usage_report`). Phase 5 needed tenant *names*, so it queried the
raw event table directly — and several of the view's event names **do not exist
in the telemetry**. `add_to_list_from_flipbook`, `order_from_flipbook`,
`order_kit`, `order_configured_item`, `view_placements` and `view_showroom_cart`
all return zero rows. The real names are `flipbook_add_to_list`,
`flipbook_add_to_order`, `add_kit_to_order`, `add_configured_item_to_order` and
`view_customer_placements`; there is **no showroom-cart event at all**.

The live catalog holds **50 distinct event names**, dumped verbatim to
`out/p5_event_catalog.txt`. Every figure in this document comes from that raw
table, not the view. This is the second defect found in the same view — Phase 2
already corrected three feature counts in it — so the view should be treated as
unreliable for anything load-bearing.

**Second correction: non-production tenants were being counted as customers.**
`demo`, `demo2`, `demo3`, `test1`, `pf_test`, `sc_test`, `ufistaging` and
`ihw_staging` are demo and staging organizations. They appear in the telemetry
and inflate reach. They are separated out below rather than removed, so the
classification can be disputed. It changes conclusions: `Commitments` looks like
4 tenants and is **2**.

conversation with the named tenants in §F.

## G. What each ranking rests on

**Rank 1 — `SemanticSearch`, 456 LOC.** The strongest candidate, and the reason is
depth rather than breadth. It reaches 12 production tenants, which sounds like a
real constituency, but those 12 generated **74 events across both of its
instrumented actions in a full year**, and the most recent was 2026-05-19 — nine
of the 12 tenants fired it once or twice, in a cluster between February and May
2026. Its two nominal port hazards, `coreml_on_device_embeddings` and
`sqlite_vec_vector_search`, both have **zero call sites**. Registry coverage:
none. Test files: 0. The evidence pattern is a feature that was switched on,
tried, and left.

**Rank 2 — `ShowroomCart`, 1,014 LOC.** Two tenants, from the `showroom_locations`
Postgres table. **No telemetry event exists for it**, so there is no usage depth
to measure — only that two tenants are configured for it. It is also the only unit
in the codebase whose purpose requires `split_view_ipad_multitasking`, a hazard
with no default web equivalent, which makes it the unit where a keep decision
carries a design decision with it. Registry coverage: none.

**Rank 3 — `Commitments`, 510 LOC.** Two production tenants, `mh` and `ufi`, and
they use it properly: 2,242 events. `ufi` last touched it 2026-06-10 and `mh` on
2026-07-27. Ranked third rather than first precisely because the attachment is
real. Notably, the component registry *references* a `commitments-view` component
and **never registers it** — so this unit is both low-use and one of the three
documented gaps in the specification.

**Rank 4 — `RepActivity`, 1,166 LOC.** The largest LOC on the ranked list and
71.8% LOGIC. 7 tenants from Postgres, **no telemetry event**, registry coverage
none, 0 tests, and coupling 0/0 that is `UNKNOWN_SWIFT_OPAQUE` rather than
measured isolation. Every signal about this unit is either absent or unmeasurable,
which is itself the finding.

**Rank 5 — `Spreadsheet Import`, 327 LOC.** Ranked here only because nothing can
be established about it. **No telemetry event, no Postgres table**, so
`tenants_touched` is `UNKNOWN` — as are `port_class`, `blast_radius` and
`reversibility`. It cannot be defended or condemned on current evidence and is
carried as invention item IR-21.

**Rank 6 — `Flipbook`, 510 LOC.** 10 production tenants and 6,053 events, still
active as of 2026-08-07 — so viewing is genuine. But the *write* paths are nearly
dead: `flipbook_add_to_list` fired **9 times across 2 tenants** and
`flipbook_add_to_order` **22 times across 3** in twelve months. One tenant, `yw`,
accounts for 4,358 of the 6,022 views. A partial decision is available here that
is not available elsewhere: the viewer is used, the ordering path is not.

**Rank 7 — `Placements`, 435 LOC.** Included for completeness and the weakest
candidate on the list. 31 production tenants, 6,855 events, active through
2026-08-07, with `cci` at 1,968 and `mli` at 977. This is a small unit with broad
adoption. It is also `MECHANICAL` under Scenario B, so it is cheap to keep in the
one scenario where keeping things is cheap.

## H. Downstream units unblocked

Removing a `LOW_USE` unit unblocks less than the LOC suggests, and the coupling
graph says why: **six of the seven ranked units are `ISOLATED`** (`Spreadsheet
Import` is `UNKNOWN`), and five report `coupling_fan_in: 0` and
`coupling_fan_out: 0`.

That zero is not a licence. All five are **100% Swift**, so their coupling is
`UNKNOWN_SWIFT_OPAQUE` — the import-graph method cannot see Swift sibling
references, and the zeros are measurement blindness rather than proven
independence. Only two of the seven have a measured edge, and they are the two
where the method can see: `SemanticSearch` at fan-in 2 / fan-out 1 is 18% Swift
and `MEASURED`; `Flipbook` at fan-in 1 / fan-out 0 is 94.7% Swift and therefore a
lower bound.

What removal *does* unblock is narrower and real:

| If removed | Hazard pressure relieved |
|---|---|
| `ShowroomCart` | `split_view_ipad_multitasking` loses its only purposeful call site; the hazard then survives only in `Global classes` and `Grid view related`, where it is incidental |
| `ShowroomCart` + `Flipbook` | `popover_presentation` drops from 8 carrying units to 6 |
| `Flipbook` | one of two `pdf_generation_or_render` consumers outside `Reporting and Email Generation` |
| `SemanticSearch` | `fmdb_sqlite_direct` loses a consumer; both of its own hazards were already unexercised |

No ranked removal unblocks a `SPINE` unit, and none changes the Scenario A/B
delta. **The kill list does not reduce the invention count.** That is worth
stating plainly: the units that are cheap to remove are not the units driving the
uncertainty.

## SELF-AUDIT

**Does any output contain a time unit, or a scale that implies one?**
No durations of work. Calendar dates appear as `last_seen` / "most recent use"
values, which are **provenance for a usage claim** — the assertion that
`SemanticSearch` was abandoned is only checkable if the date it stopped is
visible. "Events (12mo)" names the observation window. Neither is an effort
quantity, and no ranking is scored on a numeric scale.

**Does every factual row have a `source`?**
Yes. Reachability, orphan classes, orphan IB documents and not-in-project files
come from `01_CODE_CENSUS.json → 1_8_dead_code`, whose own `source` records the
commands used. Per-tenant usage comes from `scripts/kill_named.json`, generated by
`scripts/p5_named.py`, with the SQL in the script and the event catalog in
`out/p5_event_catalog.txt`. LOC, LOGIC, registry coverage and test counts come
from `03_UNIT_LEDGER.json`. Tables are generated, not typed.

**Did I report distributions where tenants differ, or did I average?**
Named every tenant rather than counting them, in §F, and gave per-tenant event
counts in `out/p5_named.txt` so concentration is visible — `yw` holds 4,358 of
6,022 `view_flipbook` events, and `cci` 1,968 of 6,855 `view_customer_placements`.
No unit is characterised by a mean. The ranking deliberately uses reach and depth
as two separate quantities, because collapsing them would have put
`SemanticSearch` (12 tenants) below `Commitments` (2) and inverted the list.

**Did I mark anything MECHANICAL that actually depends on an unmade decision?**
No port classification happens here. The reverse check applies: four ranked units
carry `port_class: REQUIRES_PRODUCT_DECISION` in Phase 3 precisely because this
kill decision precedes classification, and this document supplies the evidence
for it without making the call.

**Did I fill any field with a plausible guess rather than UNKNOWN?**
No, and three units say so. `ShowroomCart` and `RepActivity` have **no telemetry
event**, so their reach is Postgres-derived configuration rather than measured
usage, and the "Reach basis" column says which. `Spreadsheet Import` is `UNKNOWN`
on reach entirely. It would have been easy to write "low use" for all three; only
one of them has evidence of use at all.

**Did I run tooling, or did I read and approximate?**
Tooling, and it changed the answer twice. Querying the raw event table instead of
the prebuilt view revealed that six of the event names Phase 2 relied on do not
exist, and separating demo and staging organizations halved `Commitments`'
apparent tenant count. Both corrections came from running a query rather than
reading a summary. The only judgement here is the rank order, which is argued in
§G item by item so it can be re-ordered by someone who weighs depth against
breadth differently.
