Investigative Context

Declining unit prices may reflect several dynamics: competitive pricing pressure driving buyers to request discounts, a natural shift toward lower-priced product tiers, changing buyer mix within an account, or seasonal promotional activity. These patterns warrant individual account review — they are signals to investigate, not conclusions to act on.

Action: Several of these accounts are growing in dollar volume even as average unit price falls — Graybar Electric - NOGA more than doubled to $324K this quarter while its average unit price dropped 53%, suggesting a shift toward higher-volume, lower-priced items rather than a weakening relationship. Prioritize a focused mix review on the high-GMV names (Graybar, Nuvo Sales, Lamps Plus) where the relationship is clearly active but the product mix is moving down-tier.
Action: eCat carries $922K of your $90.8M in all-channel business today, but the pattern that matters is acceleration — new-buyer acquisition is rising (8 to 13 per month), 75% of it self-service, and named accounts like Nuvo Sales are compounding into six figures. The fastest path to growth is shifting more of your high-volume, already-loyal accounts (Shades of Light at 11.5% eCat share, Nuvo at 7.6%) onto the catalog for reorders; richer order-origin data in the all-channel feed would let us pinpoint exactly where eCat fits in your full channel mix.
