Behavioral Scorecard Spotlight

Platform engagement patterns across your top reps reveal distinct selling workflows (trailing 12 months). Three behavioral patterns stand out:

High Activity, Low Presentation

Sandy Glosson generates 5,092 customer-targeting events (highest on the team) and browses 2,025 products — but has only 6 customer-facing presentations on record. She's finding customers and products effectively but rarely shares content externally. With $433K in orders already flowing through pure search-and-submit, even modest presentation activity could expand her customer engagement and average order value.

Discovery-Driven Closers

Stacie Baker and Lesley Blair combine top-quartile product discovery (4,083 and 2,745 events respectively) with active customer-facing presentations (239 and 79). Their engagement patterns — browse deeply, share selectively, close consistently — correlate with the highest AOVs among volume sellers ($2,689 and $2,631). This discovery-to-presentation workflow is the behavioral signature of your highest-value selling style.

Order-Direct Pattern

Russ Jones (229 orders, $280K GMV) and Mary Miller (201 orders, $160K GMV) place orders with zero customer-facing presentations. Both maintain strong order volume through direct submission — but their AOVs ($2,143 and $1,487) trail behind presentation-active peers. The behavioral gap suggests they're filling repeat orders efficiently but may be leaving cross-sell and new-item opportunities on the table.

Your team splits into three behavioral modes: discovery-driven sellers who present and close at premium AOVs, volume-direct orderers who skip presentation entirely, and high-targeting reps who engage many customers but rarely share catalog content. The gap between these modes isn't about effort — it's about workflow. The discovery-driven pattern correlates with $800+ higher AOVs, suggesting that coaching reps toward product exploration and selective presentation could lift order values without requiring more customer outreach.
