Of 2,765 total account records, 417 have placed at least one eCat order. In the trailing 12 months, only 181 of those accounts remained active—a 43.4% retention rate that indicates more than half of previously activated buyers have stopped ordering through the platform. Meanwhile, 2,348 accounts (84.9% of your full account base) have never placed an eCat order.

Revenue Concentration Risk

Among those 181 active buyers, eCat ordering value is heavily concentrated: the top 5 accounts generated 30.7% of the $887K in trailing-12-month eCat GMV, and the top 10 accounted for 44.6%. A single account—L Design and Production LLC—represented 9.0% of all eCat order value. This level of concentration creates meaningful revenue exposure if any top account shifts ordering behavior.

What this tells you: The eCat platform has reached only 15% of your full account base, and retention among those who have tried it is under 50%. The 236 lapsed accounts represent proven buyers who have already gone through onboarding—reactivating even a fraction of them is a lower-effort path to growth than new activation. The 2,348 never-activated accounts represent a large untapped opportunity, though likely requiring targeted outreach by territory.

Single-Customer Concentration

British Columbia ($43K from 1 customer) and Trinidad & Tobago ($32K from 1 customer) each have their entire regional eCat footprint tied to a single account. If either account churns, those markets go to zero on the platform. New Jersey similarly depends on just 2 accounts for $76K in eCat value. California, by contrast, has 10 customers generating $34K—a more distributed base but lower average order value.

What this tells you: eCat ordering is distributed across at least 20 regions, with Quebec leading at $122K from 16 customers. However, several high-value regions depend on just one or two accounts, creating geographic concentration risk. Expanding the active buyer base in regions like BC, NJ, and VA—where a small number of accounts drive outsized value—would build a more resilient geographic footprint.

Channel Shift: eCat Online Now Leads Acquisition

In the trailing 12 months, the self-serve B2B Cart channel brought in 104 first-time orderers—more than double the 46 acquired through iPad reps. This shift accelerated dramatically: in March 2026, 16 of 17 first-time orderers came through eCat Online. The B2B Cart is emerging as the primary acquisition engine, complementing the rep-driven iPad channel.

What this tells you: Approximately 150 first-time buyers joined eCat in the trailing 12 months, with the self-serve B2B Cart now responsible for roughly 7 in 10 new buyer acquisitions. iPad-driven acquisition, led by Katy Tipton (10 buyers), remains important for relationship-based selling—but the growth story is online self-serve. Investing in the B2B Cart experience (discovery, onboarding, checkout) is likely to have the highest marginal return on new buyer acquisition.
