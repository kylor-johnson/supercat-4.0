75% of accounts that have ever ordered via eCat (3,420 of 4,537) have not placed an order in the trailing 12 months. Cross-Channel Repeat Purchase Signal

Looking across all ordering channels in your business system (not limited to eCat), 34.6% of new buyers return to place a second order within 90 days. The 180-day repeat rate reaches 46.8% (676 of 1,445 new buyers during the period). These all-channel figures suggest the product resonates with buyers who try it — the challenge is getting more accounts past that first order.

What this means: The eCat platform has touched 70% of your account base at some point, proving it can reach broadly. But retention is the bottleneck — only one in four eCat buyers returned in the trailing 12 months. The all-channel 34.6% repeat rate within 90 days confirms that buyers who do order tend to come back. The opportunity lies in re-engaging the 3,420 lapsed eCat buyers who already know the platform rather than relying solely on activating accounts that have never tried it.

New York: High-Density Market

New York generates nearly as much eCat GMV as North Carolina ($1.37M vs. $1.43M over the trailing 12 months) with less than one-third the customer count (44 vs. 142). That translates to roughly $31,200 in GMV per customer in NY compared to $10,100 per customer in NC — a 3× difference in per-account value. New York buyers also place more orders per customer (10.1 vs. 3.0), suggesting deeply engaged accounts that may respond well to expanded product exposure.

What this means: Revenue geography skews heavily Southeast, but New York punches far above its weight on a per-customer basis. Expanding the NY customer count — even modestly — could yield outsized GMV returns given the demonstrated per-account spending pattern. Meanwhile, states like Mississippi ($459K from just 34 customers, trailing 12 months) also show strong per-customer economics worth investigating for targeted growth.
