2.2 Behavioral Scorecard Spotlight

Platform engagement data from the trailing 12 months reveals how reps move through the selling workflow — from customer targeting, through product discovery and configuration, to presentation and order submission. Comparing these stages exposes where deals lose momentum before reaching checkout.

Widespread Configuration Gap

Across the top producers, configuration-tool usage runs well below 5% of product-browsing activity. The organization-wide ratio of configuration events to product-discovery events sits far below the benchmark threshold, indicating that reps browse products extensively but rarely bundle, customize, or configure items before moving to checkout. This pattern suppresses average order values and limits cross-sell opportunities.

Presentation Workflow Under-Used

Several reps with strong product-discovery activity rarely transition into the presentation workflow. Fletcher Flynn, for example, shows a presentation-to-configuration ratio of just 0.04 over the trailing 12 months — well below the cohort norm — suggesting configured selections are not being shared with buyers as curated presentations before order submission.

Reps who do engage the full funnel — discovery through configuration through presentation — tend to carry higher average order values. Eleanor Ward and Jennifer Whipple both exceed the $3,589 organization median, and both show meaningfully higher configuration and presentation engagement relative to their order volumes.

What this means: The largest revenue-improvement lever across the sales team is not more prospecting or more orders — it is deeper engagement with the configuration and presentation tools that already exist. Closing the configuration gap among the top five volume producers alone could materially lift average order values without requiring any additional customer acquisition.
