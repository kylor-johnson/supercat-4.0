Presentation is the widest team gap

Six of the top 20 reps have zero presentation or sharing activity (no PDF catalogs created, no item info emailed, no lists shared). Even the top rep by GMV, who submits over 1,000 orders annually, generates almost no presentation events. This suggests the team closes effectively through direct relationship selling, but the presentation and sharing tools represent an untapped layer that could strengthen buyer engagement.

What this means: The team's selling workflow is strongest in the early stages (customer targeting and product discovery) and at the close (order submission), but the middle of the funnel—building configured orders and sharing proposals with buyers—is where the most momentum is lost. Reps who do use sharing tools tend to have above-median order values, suggesting that the presentation step may contribute to larger, more considered purchases.

Archetype insight

The team's dominant rep, Melissa Roman ($16.1M, 793 orders, 98 accounts), doesn't fit a single archetype—she combines the volume of a Relationship Seller with the AOV of a Precision Closer. Her unique pattern (extremely high customer targeting, modest product discovery, minimal presentation, massive order volume) represents a workflow that works at scale for her but may not be replicable. The more instructive patterns for coaching are the three archetypes above, where behavioral adjustments in one dimension could shift outcomes.

What this means: The team has at least three distinct selling styles, each with different strengths. Rather than pushing all reps toward a single workflow, coaching investments can target each archetype's natural gap: Curated Discovery Sellers could benefit from broader account assignments, Volume Relationship Sellers from configuration training, and Precision Closers from incremental customer expansion.
