Minimal Penetration Into a Large Account Base

Only 16 of 7,084 total account records have ever placed an eCat order — an activation rate of just 0.2%. Of those 16, 7 ordered in the trailing 12 months and only 2 remain active in the last 90 days. The platform has barely scratched the surface of Buster & Punch’s dealer network. However, the 43.8% retention rate among the small cohort that has tried eCat is a positive signal: nearly half of all-time buyers are still ordering within the year, suggesting the platform delivers value once customers are onboarded.

What this means: eCat adoption is at a pre-scale stage for Buster & Punch. The 7,068 accounts that have never placed an eCat order represent an enormous untapped opportunity, but the current pace of activation is negligible. The upside is that the small cohort of eCat adopters shows healthy retention (43.8%), meaning the platform works for those who use it. The challenge is entirely an onboarding and awareness problem, not a product-satisfaction problem. Scaling from 16 to even 50 eCat buyers would represent a 3× increase while still reaching less than 1% of the account base.

Single-Account Concentration Risk

Utah accounts for $13,355 of the $25,956 trailing-12-month eCat GMV (51.5%), but that revenue comes from a single customer. Every state on this list has either 1 or 2 customers, meaning the loss of any one account would eliminate an entire region’s eCat activity. Geographic diversity exists in name only — there is no state with enough depth to sustain itself if its sole account churns.

What this means: The geographic footprint reflects early-stage adoption rather than regional market penetration. Utah’s dominance is entirely a single-account story, and California’s two accounts are split between an active buyer and a lapsed one. Until the active customer count grows past single digits, geographic analysis is less about market strategy and more about understanding which reps have successfully onboarded accounts in which territories. The Western U.S. skew may reflect current rep coverage patterns rather than underlying demand.
