Three Questions

We classify by selling motion, not by eCat usage:

  • What do they sell? — product type and price point ($3/unit vs $9,100/unit)
  • How do they sell it? — specification into projects, brand-building through dealers, multi-channel distribution, or commodity replenishment
  • Who buys from them? — interior designers, showroom dealers, retail chains, category managers
Segment boundaries:
• Luxury ↔ Premium: Designers specify products into projects vs. buyers choose a brand through dealers
• Premium ↔ Mid-Market: Trade-primary brand-building vs. omnichannel distribution (trade + retail + ecommerce + contractor)
• Mid-Market ↔ Volume: Moderate per-piece pricing ($55+) vs. commodity-adjacent bulk ($33 and below)

What They Are

High-end furniture and lighting companies selling products above $500/unit through controlled trade channels. Their end customer is an interior designer, architect, or hospitality procurement team.

How They Sell

  • Relationship-driven field sales through rep networks and showrooms
  • High Point, Dallas Market, private showroom presentations
  • Custom/configured orders (COM fabrics, custom finishes, bespoke sizing)
  • Orders originate as project specifications, not impulse purchases
  • Most do NOT sell direct-to-consumer with public checkout

Who Buys

  • Interior designers specifying for residential projects
  • Architects specifying for commercial/hospitality builds
  • Independently owned showroom dealers
  • Hospitality procurement teams (hotels, restaurants, luxury developers)

Key Commercial Truth

These companies' orders don't consummate in eCat. A designer specs a $6,000 chandelier → rep enters the project in eCat → order goes to HQ → HQ audits, configures, enters into ERP → ships weeks later. eCat is the presentation and project-capture tool, never the transaction system.

What They Are

Mid-to-upper-market brands ($150–$700/unit) building brand recognition with consumers while keeping trade/dealer channels as the primary revenue driver. Multi-tier pricing typical.

How They Sell

  • Authorized dealer networks + growing DTC presence
  • Trade programs with tiered pricing; many do contract/hospitality
  • Select marketplace/curated retail (Lumens, Perigold) — rarely mass Amazon/Wayfair
  • Reps manage territory coverage and dealer relationships
  • Brand marketing to consumers (social, lifestyle, Shopify stores)

Who Buys

  • Dealers and showrooms (primary)
  • Interior designers
  • Direct consumers via website (growing but secondary)
  • Hospitality/contract specifiers

What They Are

Lighting, furniture, and home goods companies selling $30–$150 products through as many channels as possible. Compete on availability, breadth of distribution, and price competitiveness.

How They Sell

  • Everywhere: own site, Amazon, Wayfair, Home Depot, Lowe's, showrooms, distributors
  • High SKU counts (thousands of products)
  • Dealer portals for wholesale; product feeds for online retailers
  • Mix of decorative/residential and builder/commercial

Who Buys

  • Lighting showrooms and electrical distributors
  • Online marketplace shoppers (growing fast)
  • Builders, contractors, and electricians
  • Home Depot / Lowe's as retail partners

Key Commercial Truth

Distribution arms race — availability and catalog breadth beat exclusivity. eCat is one wholesale channel among many; most volume may never touch the iPad. Reps need prompts that flex between dealer, distributor, retail, and online motions.

What They Are

High-volume, low-unit-price distribution (<$30/unit). Housewares, tabletop, bulbs, giftware, low-cost decor. Buyer is a category manager at a chain or purchasing agent.

How They Sell

  • POs from retail chains and regional distributors
  • Trade shows (AmericasMart, housewares/gift shows)
  • Category management: planograms, shelf space, MOQs, container-direct
  • High velocity, low per-line value

Who Buys

  • Category managers at big-box retailers
  • Regional distributors and wholesalers
  • Convenience/drug/grocery chain buyers
  • Mass marketplace shoppers

Column Key

$/Unit
Best price: invoiced unit price when available (39 orgs), else 2σ-scrubbed catalog median
IQR
Middle 50% of prices across orgs in that segment (25th–75th percentile)
Orders
Total eCat orders ever submitted (all time). Proxy for platform depth.
T12M
Trailing 12 months of orders. Shows current activity vs. historical.
AOV
Average Order Value. Big = project orders; small = replenishment.
PCodes
Distinct price levels (Dealer, Designer, Net, Retail). More = more complex channel.
Customers
Customer records loaded in eCat. Ranges from 34 to 92,000+.
Terr
Territory assignments. Zero = not configured, not proof of no coverage.
Users
eCat user accounts (reps + portal buyers). 8K+ usually means B2B buyer logins.
DCs
Distribution centers. Most orgs have 0 — fulfillment happens outside eCat.
