## Migration Driver: What You're Correcting

**user_rate_normalization**

Magnussen Home signed in 2023 at $20/user for additional users beyond their contracted base of 25 — a rate that was below the $25 book rate. With an average of 63 billable users and only 10 included in T1, they're carrying 53 excess users. At the new 2026 graduated rate structure ($25/$22/$20/$18), the user charge moves from approximately $1,060/month (53 × $20) to $1,134/month — an increase of $74/month on user charges. The T1 platform base also corrects from $725 to $749 (+$24). Combined, the delta is $138/month (+7.9%). This is a 2026 standardization applied across the full install base — every account on a below-book user rate is receiving the same correction.

## Likely Objections — With Responses

### "Why is this happening now?"

> "We've been working through a full pricing standardization this year — the first time we've applied a consistent structure across all accounts. You've been on a below-book user rate since you signed in 2023. We're bringing every account onto the same structure this year."

### "My original rate was part of a deal — I earned that."

> "Your original rate was set at sign-on — not a permanent structure, just where we landed at the time. What we're doing now is applying the same graduated rate to everyone. You're not being singled out; the same correction is going across the full install base."

### "Why us? Are other accounts getting this too?"

> "Yes — 100% of the install base is moving to the same structure. You're not being singled out. Accounts at every tier and tenure level are getting the same correction. Your number reflects your user count and tier, nothing else."

### "This is a 7.9% increase. That's not something we can absorb easily."

> "I understand that. At $138/month, this is one of the smaller deltas across the migration — and the effective date gives you two billing cycles to plan for it. If timing is the concern, we can delay the effective date by up to 30 days at my discretion. If there's a specific budget concern, let's talk about what we can do."

### "What happens if we say no?"

> Option 1 (firm): "This is a standard migration we're completing across the full install base. The effective date is 2026-07-17. I want to work with you to make the transition smooth, but the pricing structure itself is the same for everyone."
> Option 2 (accommodation available): "We're not looking for an adversarial conversation. If you have a specific budget constraint — a fiscal year challenge — let's talk about what we can do to bridge the gap. I can offer a phase-in or a one-time credit. What I can't do is hold a legacy rate indefinitely."
