## Executive summary

- **Where we play**: B2B wholesale tools for furniture, lighting, and home-décor manufacturers — a market converging on Good/Better/Best subscription packaging with platform fees as the dominant value capture. See [`02_who_we_serve.md`](02_who_we_serve.md) for the industry shape.
- **What we sell against, in our customers' words**: Velocity drag (48 mentions), missed opportunities (41), error & rework (36), visibility gaps (36), manual process burden (34), configuration complexity (16), inconsistent messaging (12). Source: 89–110 customer-facing meetings across 45 companies, FY2025.
- **Five direct competitors**: AmpTab, Pepperi, WizCommerce, MarketTime, RepZio/ShopZio. Plus 8 adjacent (Candid Wholesale, B2B Wave, Endless Commerce, Onsight, App4Sales, Geckoboard, Equals, QuickSight).
- **The one to watch**: **WizCommerce** — AI-first, fast implementation, $8M raised, same ICP, aggressive pricing. Marked High threat.
- **The most-encountered**: **AmpTab** — same vertical, transparent G/B/B pricing, "unlimited users at every tier" as the headline message.
- **What we win on**: Vertical depth in furniture/lighting; deepest CPQ in the competitive set; Order & Invoice Tracking included in T2 (AmpTab gates equivalent at $3,000 Pro); Sales Intelligence + Insights Layer at T3; price transparency vs. quote-only competitors; Y1 effective cost within 15% of AmpTab at every tier when setup fees amortized.
- **What we lose on**: AmpTab's "unlimited users" message at sticker; WizCommerce's AI capabilities and sub-30-day implementation; MarketTime's network effects (300K buyers, 6,500 brands); Pepperi's enterprise breadth (multi-language, DSD, merchandising).
- **What we don't yet quantify** (and the doc says so explicitly): empirical win/loss by competitor, the status-quo / "we can build it" / Excel non-purchase competitor, ERP/horizontal-platform encroachment (NetSuite, Acumatica, Salesforce B2B Commerce, Shopify B2B), competitor scale & motion table, and customer-voiced competitive language by named vendor.

## How they're packaged (the structural patterns)

Source: [`skills/monetization_refresh_2026/11_synthesis/2026-01-28__competitive_packaging_audit__m3_input__v1.md`](../skills/monetization_refresh_2026/11_synthesis/2026-01-28__competitive_packaging_audit__m3_input__v1.md), Patterns 1–6.

### Packaging models in use

| Model | Vendors | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Good/Better/Best tiers** | AmpTab, Pepperi, **SuperCat (2026)** | **Dominant** — 2 of 3 transparent-pricing vendors |
| Modular products | WizCommerce | Emerging (AI-first vendors) |
| Per-user flat | RepZio | Legacy |
| Quote-only | MarketTime, WizCommerce | Common but competitively disadvantaged |

### Value-metric consensus

| Value metric | Vendors | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform fee (flat monthly)** | AmpTab, Pepperi, WizCommerce (est.) | **Dominant** |
| **Per-user** | RepZio, Onsight, **SuperCat (T1/T2/T3)** | Used as expansion lever |
| **Transaction pools** | Pepperi, Candid Wholesale | Emerging |
| SKU-based | Endless Commerce, B2B Wave | Adjacent only |
| GMV-based | Candid Wholesale | "Success tax" perception |

### Tier-fence themes (what differentiates Base → Mid → Top across the market)

- **Base**: Catalog + ordering + basic reporting (table stakes everywhere)
- **Mid**: Integrations + advanced commerce + CPQ/configuration + territory management
- **Top**: Analytics/BI + AI/intelligence + multi-channel + enterprise operations

This pattern matches SuperCat's T1 → T2 → T3 fences nearly exactly. See [`03_how_we_make_money.md`](03_how_we_make_money.md).

### "Unlimited users" — the AmpTab message

AmpTab's most potent message is "unlimited users at every tier." Pepperi also includes unlimited users at all tiers. **SuperCat is the only direct competitor with billable users as the primary value metric** (RepZio uses pure per-user pricing — also an outlier in the opposite direction). This is a real competitive tension and the response narrative matters: total cost at actual team size, expansion-aligned pricing, no phantom-seat tax.

## Where we're vulnerable

| Vulnerability | Who exploits it | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **"Unlimited users"** | AmpTab (all tiers), Pepperi (all tiers) | Total-cost-of-ownership narrative. At 15 users, SuperCat T1 is +$374/mo vs AmpTab Starter; at 50 users, SuperCat T3 is *–$455/mo* vs AmpTab Pro. The crossover happens around 30–40 users. The expansion economics fund product investment AmpTab can't match. |
| **AI capabilities** | WizCommerce (Kai sales assistant, AI search, AI recommendations, WizStudio AI imagery) | Roadmap question — outside pricing/packaging scope. The Insights Layer is the closest counter-positioning today (analytical intelligence vs. generative AI). |
| **Implementation speed** | WizCommerce ("sub-30-day implementation, low-cost") | Position depth and quality for complex catalog/data work in furniture/lighting. The Data Assessment ($1,500 creditable) is the structural answer to "how fast can you go live?" |
| **Network effects** | MarketTime (6,500 brands, 300K retail buyers, 350 sales agencies, 7,000 salespeople, 2.7M orders/year) | Different value proposition entirely. SuperCat is a platform; MarketTime is a marketplace. Reframe — don't try to out-network them. |
| **Enterprise breadth** | Pepperi (multi-language, multi-currency, DSD/route accounting, merchandising, planogram, replenishment) | We are vertical depth, not horizontal breadth. Lose-fast on enterprise prospects who need multi-language storefronts or van-sales/DSD. |
| **Sub-$500/mo simplicity** | RepZio ($25/user/mo, no tiers, no feature gating) | Different segment. RepZio is for small accounts that need a rep app, not a platform. SuperCat T1 at $749 buys catalog + ordering + browse + admin + reports; RepZio at $25/user buys an iPad app. |
| **Marketing polish** | WizCommerce | Real gap. WizCommerce's positioning, web presence, and category narrative are visibly more modern. Product investment is offsetting; marketing investment is the structural ask. |

## Per-competitor brief (the response playbook)

Source: [`skills/monetization_refresh_2026/11_synthesis/2026-02-25__competitive_price_benchmarking__d004a_exercise2__v1.md`](../skills/monetization_refresh_2026/11_synthesis/2026-02-25__competitive_price_benchmarking__d004a_exercise2__v1.md), drafted competitive response playbook.

### AmpTab (most common; Medium threat)

**Their pitch**: "Same industry, transparent G/B/B pricing, unlimited users at every tier."

**Our response**: "Comparable features, lower total cost at scale, vertical depth for furniture/lighting." Lead with Y1 TCO (setup fees amortized), then the Order & Invoice Tracking gap (we include it at T2; they gate it at $3,000 Pro), then the deeper CPQ.

**If they raise "unlimited users"**: "Our included base covers your team. You pay for growth, not phantom seats. Compare total cost at your actual team size, not sticker price."

### WizCommerce (High threat — the one to watch)

**Their pitch**: AI-first; sub-30-day implementation; aggressive pricing; $8M raised; 500+ customers.

**Our response**: "AI is a feature, not a platform. We run your commerce operation end-to-end including configurable products." Hammer the CPQ gap (they don't have it). Hammer the implementation depth question for complex furniture/lighting data.

**Where we lose**: Prospects who weight AI capabilities heavily and don't have configurable products in their catalog.

### Pepperi (Medium threat — enterprise deals)

**Their pitch**: Enterprise-grade; unlimited users; multi-storefront; multi-language; DSD/route accounting; merchandising; transaction pools.

**Our response**: "Same capabilities at 60% less, and we're built for furniture/lighting depth — not generic horizontal B2B." Hammer the price (Pepperi Corporate at 25 users = $3,450/mo vs SuperCat T2 = $1,545/mo). Hammer vertical fit.

**Where we lose**: Multi-language requirements; DSD/van-sales requirements; merchandising/planogram requirements. Lose fast.

### MarketTime (Low-Medium threat — incumbent volume)

**Their pitch**: Largest network — 6,500 brands, 300K buyers, 350 agencies, $5B+ annual orders. Network access is the moat.

**Our response**: "MarketTime is a marketplace; we're a platform. If your goal is to access their buyer network, that's a marketing decision. If your goal is to run your commerce operation, we're the answer." Lean on price transparency (they're quote-only).

**Where we lose**: Customers whose primary value driver is *being on MarketTime's marketplace* — that's a different decision.

### RepZio / ShopZio (Low threat — different segment)

**Their pitch**: Simple per-user pricing ($25/user/mo); ANDMORE ownership = trade-show bundle.

**Our response**: "RepZio is a rep tool; SuperCat is a commerce platform. No online catalog, no buyer self-serve, no analytics." Show the feature gap. Right-size on price (a 25-user RepZio deployment = $625/mo for the rep app only; SuperCat T1 at $749 buys the rep app + catalog + browse + admin).

**Where we lose**: Small accounts that already have an ANDMORE relationship and only need a rep app.

### "We can build it" / status quo (frequency unknown — see Open Intelligence Questions)

**Their pitch**: "Our IT team / a dev shop / Shopify can build this for us."

**Our response**: "Total build cost exceeds 5 years of SuperCat. You're buying ongoing product investment, not a one-time build." Show implementation complexity (catalog, options, kits, sync, offline iPad, ERP integration) + ongoing maintenance + the value-moments roadmap.

**Where we lose**: Prospects with strong internal engineering and a CTO who values control over time-to-value. Common with founder-led larger manufacturers.

## Open intelligence questions

The doc is honest about what it cannot yet claim. Each item below is a real piece of competitive intelligence work; tracked in the foundation backlog as `followon-*` todos.

1. **Win/loss data by competitor.** Today's advantage/vulnerability tables are *anecdotal*, not *empirical*. We do not currently quantify how often we win when AmpTab is in the deal vs WizCommerce vs status quo. Closing this needs a Fathom + HubSpot deal-mining pass — the Fathom corpus already classifies pain/objection/impact clusters but does not slice by named competitor.
2. **Status quo / "we can build it" / Excel competitor.** The most common loss path is almost certainly *no purchase*, not a competitor purchase. Today this gets a single bullet in the response playbook. It deserves its own analysis: what fraction of qualified prospects (in-niche by behavioral segment fit) don't buy at all, what they're using instead, and what closes that gap.
3. **ERP / horizontal-platform encroachment.** NetSuite, Acumatica, Salesforce B2B Commerce, Shopify B2B as adjacent threats. Not in either source today. These don't show up in the audit because they're not packaged like the named direct competitors — but they show up in deals (the "we can build it on Shopify" pattern).
4. **Competitor scale & motion table.** Partial only — WizCommerce ($8M raised, 500+ customers); MarketTime (6,500 brands, 300K buyers, $5B+ orders, 2.7M orders/year); AmpTab "mid-market" with named clients (Coaster, Progressive, Hillsdale, Riverside, Jofran). A 5-row competitor scale table (revenue range, employees, funding, customer count, recent moves) belongs here, even with "unknown" cells.
5. **Time decay.** Both source files are Jan/Feb 2026 snapshots. Nine-plus months have passed. This doc commits to **quarterly refresh** — but the next refresh has not yet happened. Treat the figures here as anchored to early-2026 and validate before quoting in customer conversations.
6. **Customer-voiced competitive language.** The Fathom corpus in [`reports/state_of_industry/section2_evidence/`](../reports/state_of_industry/section2_evidence/) clusters buyer friction generally, not by named competitor. Slicing the corpus to surface "what prospects say about WizCommerce" or "what they say about AmpTab" would strengthen the playbook proof points materially. Sliceable, not yet sliced.
