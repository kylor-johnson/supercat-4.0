Who uses each surface, what they get, and what the data says we had wrong

Seven personas and 31 jobs, placed on the surfaces we already run. Then measured against production — which split one persona in two, cut the flagship build's launch population by a fifth, and moved two jobs out of the blocked column.

What this changes

four claims, ranked by consequence

Ranked by what each costs us to ignore. The first changes who we are building for; the second changes what we should build next; the third confirms how much to build; the fourth is the only one that is a conversation rather than a sprint.

01

“Independent sales rep” is two personas, and the one we designed for is 353 records

Of 4,026 rep records active on the iPad, 2,806 wrote no order in 90 days — and they are not dormant: 368 of them used the device on 30 or more separate days. That is a catalog, price and availability user who never submits through us. Meanwhile 353 records wrote 86% of all orders and $143.4M of value. An invoiced-dollars account panel answers a question the larger group is not asking.

Build once is right — and two of the four numbers we argued it with were wrong

Where it lands on the product

persona × surface

The same 32 jobs, read the other way. Everything rep-facing is one new component embedded in the offline iPad app; everything else lands on a web surface we already run. The pattern worth noticing: the two internal back-office personas straddle the Portal and Admin — they read in one and act in the other — while the buyer never touches either.

Where it should land

persona group → jobs → today → suggested

The register was built from the product outward — it took the surfaces we have and asked who uses them. This runs the chain the other way: start from the persona, derive the jobs, then ask where each job should live and compare that with where it lives now. Twelve of the thirty rows have no home anywhere today. Three exist but are in the wrong place, which is the more interesting problem, because it means someone already built something and pointed it at the wrong audience.

Wrong home — 1 of 3

Buyer purchase history is in the Portal

It runs in the Sales Portal’s buyer half at 6 orgs. Buyers do not live in the Portal — 18,057 of them live in eCat Online, in the catalog and cart they already open. Putting a dealer’s own history where the dealer already is changes the reach by two orders of magnitude and costs no new build.

Wrong home — 2 of 3

Rep adoption is measured with the wrong instrument

The only existing mechanism is an iPad CRM-logging pilot at 7 orgs, where reps tap Call / Visit / Email. The job is “are the seats we sold being used” — logins against seats licensed, in the Portal, where the person asking already works. Both fields exist. And it must show orders too: 2,806 reps log in and write nothing.

Wrong home — 3 of 3

Fading accounts live in a report the owner cannot open

“Accounts fading” exists as an Insightful concept, not as anything an owner can reach. It is $112.9M across 23,243 accounts and it is the highest-leverage thing an owner decides with. The owner’s Portal seat is thin by design — this is one of the two things that belongs in it.

Entitlement, and the gap between billed and enabled

a byproduct, and it is money

The register states that feature gates live only in application config. That is true of the fine-grained gates and false of plan-level commercial entitlement, which is fully queryable. Reading entitlement against enablement turns up a gap that is nobody's persona problem and everybody's revenue problem.

25
Orgs running Cart, not billed for it
Cart is enabled at 55 orgs; 32 hold a Cart plan. At $295/mo that is roughly $88,500 a year.
14
Orgs Portal-enabled, not billed
50 orgs carry the Sales Portal flag; 37 pay the $395/mo Portal plan. One pays and is not enabled.
36 / 37
Paying Portal clients with a feed
Portal buyers are almost entirely at fed clients, which is why the feed gap barely touches them.
5
Orgs on the new tiering
T1/T2/T3 exist and are almost unused; 97 orgs sit on the legacy iPad plan.

Two things follow. Commercially, there is a reconciliation to run between what clients are entitled to and what is switched on. Analytically, entitlement is now a usable input — it is what let us put a real ceiling under the buyer-history decision instead of an estimate. Q060 · Q061 · Q062

What we are not building

and why, so it does not come back
Better supported than before

Per-segment versions of anything

Segment explains under 11% of the variance in every structural driver measured. Build one layer, parameterise on the client's own shape.

Declined, better reason

The rep agency principal

590 active reps across 42 orgs, and 465 of them have no company name recorded at all. The blocker is absence of the entity, not the messiness of 11,799 strings.

Suppressed, not approximated

Four jobs without the invoice feed

Down from six: order status has moved out. Where the question is what really happened commercially, order data is no substitute — and only 44 clients have a live feed.

No data, no estimate

Commission, margin, cash, satisfaction

Real jobs; the schema fails them. A rep is paid on what ships, and the system they use daily cannot tell them what they earned.

An invoiced-dollars panel

…for the 2,806 reps who write nothing

New. They are heavy users of catalog, price and stock. Building them a revenue panel answers a question they are not asking.

Not without a policy

Any topline off raw order totals

15 orders carry 24.2% of all recorded value and one of them is $464M. Toplines need an explicit outlier rule first.
