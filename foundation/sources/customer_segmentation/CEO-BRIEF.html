Who uses each surface, what they care about, and what we should put in front of them

4,058 reps sell on the iPad, 87,927 dealer buyers are enabled on eCat Online with 21,260 active, and about 2,274 sales leaders, ops and customer-service people work in the Sales Portal — three populations that share almost no users. Seven personas across them hold thirty-one jobs, each with one number that says whether the job got done. Build the analytics layer once, parameterised on the size and shape of each client's own data, and put each piece on the surface where the person already works — which for reps means a web component inside the iPad, not a portal they don't open.

Surface 1eCat iPad — offline, in the field

The install-base centre of gravity, and the only surface built for no connectivity. Reps carry catalog, customers, pricing and inventory locally, and write orders on it.

  • Independent sales rep PER-014,058 active · 146 orgs
  • Rep agency principal PER-02617 active in flagged rep groups
  • Internal admins on the iPad PER-03377 active

Independent sales rep — 4,058

An account panel inside the iPad, opened from the customer already on screen. No second app, no separate login.

  • Invoiced net for this account, last 12 months against prior JTBD-011New
  • Open order value and last order dateNew
  • Categories bought last year and not this yearNew
  • Accounts down more than a set percentage over 90 days JTBD-014New
  • A territory total that matches the assigned book JTBD-012Fix
  • Selling offline — catalog, customers, pricing, orders JTBD-013Exists
  • Order status a dealer can rely on JTBD-015Blocked

Decline runs against the account's own history, so a lumpy project buyer isn't flagged every time it finishes a job. Ship and carrier data sits in the client's ERP and never reaches us. Quota is not buildable as a shared feature — quota data exists in one client org, commitment reports in one, placement reports in three.

Why these numbers

Reps are paid on what ships, not what they write, and their book is a territory rather than the company. The recorded failure is trust: pick a territory, still see company-wide totals, rebuild in Excel.

Blockers

Territory coverage caps who sees any of it — see the component section. The invoice feed decides whether these are invoiced dollars or a labelled order-based stand-in.

Rep agency principal — 617

Nothing this cycle. The agency's whole book, which sub-rep is carrying the line, and renewal evidence are all Blocked.

There is no concept of an agency in the system. The only link from a person to their firm is a free-text company name typed 11,873 different ways — anything built on it would merge two agencies that spelled their name differently and split one that didn't, then hand a principal a confident wrong number in the meeting that decides their renewal.

The buyer-facing surface in three modes, on the same catalog spine the rep sees.

Dealer buyer — the largest population on any surface we run

In the catalog and cart they already use.

  • Their own entitled price, on every product viewed JTBD-083Exists
  • Reorder what they bought before, without calling JTBD-081Exists
  • What they ordered and what they were billed JTBD-082Exists
  • Stock with its snapshot age shown on it JTBD-084New
  • Their own account performance over time JTBD-086Config

Cart is entitled in 58 orgs. The inventory timestamp exists on every import and is simply never shown, which is how a buyer promises a delivery we can't make. Purchase history reaches 2 orgs today; switching it on across portal orgs reaches roughly 13,800 more active buyers — the largest reach in this document for the least engineering.

Why these numbers

Every buyer job is written from their side but justified by what our client gains — orders captured, calls deflected, share of wallet held. Anything good for the buyer and neutral for our client is left out, which is why cross-manufacturer price comparison is not on the list.

Blockers

Purchase history is held on a client-risk call, not on engineering.

Also on Sales Portal

Buyers are most of the Portal's live traffic, handled in that section. They never cross into the rep views — a buyer account cannot reach them by any configuration.

VP Sales and sales ops — 2,365 admin records

A defensible topline and the tools to stand behind it, in the Portal; the permission and import screens in Admin, where the fix happens.

  • Invoiced net for the period, company-wide, with prior year and backlog JTBD-031Exists
  • An export that matches what the screen showed JTBD-035Fix
  • Reps active in 30 days against seats licensed JTBD-032Config
  • Why a given user can't see their book JTBD-033New
  • Whether last night's import landed JTBD-034New

The topline needs honest labelling, not rebuilding. Export reconciliation is a defect fix and gates trust in every other number on the surface. Team usage is already computed and switched off — the flag is on for 7 of 257 orgs.

Why these numbers

They own the selling system rather than a book, and the week ends badly when they report a total upward and can't say where it came from.

Blockers

None structural. Rep activity, coverage and quote-to-submit discipline need no ERP feed, so this persona is servable in full even at clients who send us no invoices.

Customer service and order entry — named as a primary portal user, never counted

One account's whole story, fast — the Portal's existing customer and order history view is already the best fit in the product for this.

  • Full order and invoice history for one account JTBD-041Exists
  • Keying an order without getting the price level wrong JTBD-042Exists
  • Orders that will fail, caught before they do JTBD-044New
  • A ship date they can defend JTBD-043Blocked

Resolved-on-first-contact is their number, and they are better served by widening coverage of what exists than by new screens. Order failures aren't recorded anywhere today, so the metric can only be inferred from support tickets.

Why these numbers

The queue sets the day; they are measured on speed and accuracy, not revenue.

Blockers

No customer-service role exists in the system — they sit inside the 2,365 admin records, so the population is UNKNOWN rather than estimated.

Owner / exec — consumes conclusions, not screens

A thin Portal subset — the topline and the risk list, nothing more.

  • One true topline with its provenance attached JTBD-061Exists
  • Accounts quietly slipping, in dollars at risk JTBD-062New
  • Whether the sales organisation is working JTBD-063Config

The deep version of the topline already ships as a report rather than a screen, deliberately: an owner who logs in twice a year does not need a dashboard. Account decline needs a per-account baseline; without an invoice feed it ships as a labelled order-based view rather than not at all.

Why these numbers

They own the business, not the selling system, and usually receive the number from someone who logged in.

Product and merchandising — acts in Admin, needs evidence from the Portal

A catalog-health view in Admin, where the fix happens, with sell-through evidence in the Portal.

  • Active items missing images, prices or taxonomy JTBD-053New
  • What actually sold, by item, in units and dollars JTBD-051New
  • Whether a new introduction landed JTBD-054New

Catalog health is the cheapest genuinely new job in the register and needs no new data. "Did the new range land" needs a launch date on a product — one nullable column that doesn't exist yet.

Why these numbers

Judged on whether the assortment sells, and the week before market is when a broken product record costs real money.

Blockers

The weakest-evidenced persona kept: the work is demonstrably being done, but no role identifies who does it. First to cut if customers don't confirm it.

The rep componentWeb, embedded in the offline iPad app

Why embedded rather than native. The iPad already carries a web bridge at 36 call sites, and the Sales Portal is already embedded inside the iPad in the field today under its own request header. An established pattern in the codebase, not a new architecture.

Why the iPad rather than the Sales Portal. The portal surface a rep would use for these numbers is switched on for zero organisations — the flag resolves to nine internal SuperCat usernames, and the wider reports feature reaches six clients. Reps have never been served there. Meanwhile 4,058 of them are on the iPad, and the work happens where there is no connectivity. Rep analytics in the Portal builds a correct screen reps cannot open where they sell. Rep-facing analytics ship in the iPad component; the Sales Portal stays leadership, customer service and the buyer half. Reps saying otherwise would overturn it, and market is the cheap place to ask.

What it shows. One scrollable panel: invoiced net for this account against last year, open orders, last order date, what they've stopped buying, a quiet-account flag. It reads only from a pre-aggregated extract of roughly 6–8 MB synced with the catalog, and is read-only, which removes conflict handling entirely. It never blocks on the network — a panel that spins on a market floor breaks the one thing that already works. Every number carries its age, and an empty territory scope shows nothing rather than the whole company.

It launches to about 667 of 4,058 reps — 16% of the active base, across 20 client orgs.

Never showing a rep someone else's book means it can only run where the client maintains a territory file, and 122 of 145 orgs with active reps have none. Of the 667 who qualify, 618 are also at clients who send invoices, so they get real dollars rather than a labelled stand-in.

Not an argument against building it — nothing is displaced and the calculation work is reusable. It is an argument for fixing territory files alongside it, because that unlocks more rep value than the component does, and it is a client conversation rather than engineering.

What we are not building

Per-segment versions of anything. Only 4 of 31 jobs vary by the kind of client, and those four vary on how many products, territories and price codes an org has — not on how it sells. A job doesn't need to know a client is a luxury specification brand; it needs to know they have thirty-five price codes. Build once, parameterise on the org's own shape.

The rep agency principal. Not servable until an agency exists as a real structure, and the cost is normalising 11,873 typed-in company names, not the schema.

Six jobs that need the invoice feed. Where the question is what really happened commercially — a defensible topline, the owner's true number, agency renewal value, where an order actually is — order data is not a substitute and gets suppressed rather than approximated. Seven other jobs degrade to a labelled order-based view and ship. The feed is present at 38 of 109 clients.

Anything we can't compute. Commission, margin, cash and collections, satisfaction: real jobs, no data, no estimates. Commission is the notable one — a rep is paid on what ships, and the system they use daily can't tell them what they've earned.

Decisions

1Confirm reps get their analytics on the iPad, not the Sales Portal. Wrong toward the iPad costs a wrapper — the calculations survive. Wrong toward the Portal costs the wrapper and leaves reps unserved where they work, then forces the iPad build anyway. Settle before committing; asking reps at market is the cheap way.
2Turn on purchase history for dealer buyers? A configuration change reaching roughly 13,800 additional active buyers, competing with nothing for engineering time. Held on a commercial question: showing a buyer their own history can expose what tier they're on and what they buy, which is our client's call about their own customers. Two clients already run it.
3Say no to the rep agency principal this cycle? Recommendation on file is yes — say it explicitly rather than shipping something that merges two agencies and looks authoritative.
4Run a client data push — invoice feeds and territory files? The two constraints gating the most value, neither of them engineering work. 71 of 109 clients send no invoice feed, degrading 13 of the 31 jobs. 122 of 145 orgs with active reps have no territory file, capping the rep component at 16% of reps. Together they unlock more than the entire build list.

AppendixMethod, and what this doesn't establish

Client segments are stamped judgment, not a computation

The v4.0 selling-motion segments have been described as derived from our own data. They are not. A rule over the four defining fields — average order value, price-code count, customer count, order volume — reproduces the stamped label 38.5% of the time against a 34.9% majority-class baseline across 109 clients, and caps at 50.0% even when thresholds are fitted to the answer key. The segments came from selling-motion judgment carried forward from v3.2; the data confirmed them, it did not produce them. That does not weaken them — stamped judgment about observed selling behaviour is legitimate — but it changes the operating rule: the authority is a roster lookup, not a recalculation, and a fresh quarter of orders does not re-segment anyone. Two foundation documents carried the wrong lineage claim and were corrected on 2026-08-26, prior versions archived. taxonomy/account-segments.md §0

Why a prospect cannot carry a segment label

Everything that distinguishes selling motion exists only after a company is a customer. Mechanical feature extraction from company websites reached 33.3% against a 34.5% baseline — no better than guessing. A person reading whole sites reaches roughly 53%. The precise claim: mechanical extraction cannot predict a segment, holistic reading gets to about half, and neither is good enough to label a prospect — not the broader "public data can't predict segment," which overstates it. The worked case: a brand that plainly sells to the trade scored zero on the trade test because it says "designer resources" instead of "to the trade."taxonomy/prospect-archetypes.md §5

Honest limits

  • No customer has been interviewed. The thirty-one jobs are built from platform evidence — populations, feature-gate states, code paths, the portal spec, the platform anatomy — and prior persona work. Grounded in observed behaviour, not conversation with a rep or a buyer. That validation is the natural next piece of work.
  • No usage evidence exists for the rep surface. It has never been enabled for anyone, so there is no telemetry, no complaint stream, no baseline. Every design assumption in the component section is untested.
  • Two personas cannot be counted. Customer service and product/merchandising have no role in the system that identifies them; their populations are UNKNOWN rather than estimated.
  • Insightful 4.0 is cited only as evidence of what we can compute — account decline with dollars at risk, price-realisation leakage, rep activity, coverage and quote-to-submit discipline already run in that factory against live client data. It is a generated report, not a surface, and is not proposed as one. The rep-facing operator built on it is early beta and nothing above depends on it.
