Three things make this substantive rather than a tidier list.

1. Persona is a third axis, orthogonal to segment

The prior foundation context had no persona concept at all. It hung jobs off segments — “the JTBD per segment … are hypothesized from usage data, not confirmed by customers,” with the worked example “let buyers order without calling a rep” attached to Commerce-Active, a segment. Jobs were nested inside the segmentation. _archive/02_who_we_serve_2026-08-27_pre-persona-axis.md

They are now separated. A segment is a property of the client organisation; a persona is a behavioural role with jobs. They cross-tabulate and neither predicts the other — do not read a persona off a segment, or a segment off a persona. That rule is now stated in the runtime file every agent loads. CEO_SYSTEM_CONTEXT.md § Persona — the third axis

2. The buyer persona was missing entirely, and is the largest

PER-08 was not in the original scope. It is now the largest user population SuperCat has on any surface — 87,927 enabled buyer records, 17,532 active in ninety days. It sits on a different axis again: these people do not work for a SuperCat client, they buy from one. Every buyer job is therefore written from the buyer’s side but justified by what our client gains — orders captured, calls deflected, share of wallet held. A job good for the buyer and neutral for our client is cut, which is why “compare this manufacturer’s prices to others” is excluded outright.

Reps and buyers are also near-disjoint: only 593 users are active on both iPad and eCat Online, and should_show_customers_tab requires customer_number.blank? — a buyer cannot reach the rep views by any configuration. They share no session and no mental model. personas/PER-08-dealer-buyer.md

3. PER-02 cannot be served at all today

The rep agency principal is a real population — 617 iPad-active users inside rep groups flagged by user_types.primary_rep_group (142 of 1,980 user types, 39 orgs). But there is no agency entity in the schema. The only link from a user to their firm is org_users.company_name, a free-text field carrying 11,873 distinct values.

All four PER-02 jobs require aggregating across sub-reps, which requires knowing who the sub-reps are. An “agency view” built on free-text company names would silently merge distinct agencies that typed their name differently and split single agencies that did not — producing a number that looks authoritative and is wrong. That is a suppress case, not a degrade case. The cost is the name-normalisation migration over 11,873 values, not the schema. product/surface-mapping.md §4

The finding that reframes the ask

The register is per persona, as you asked. The question underneath the ask — whether the analytics layer also has to differ per segment — is now answered, and answered in the negative.

Only 4 of 31 jobs vary by Account Segment. Twenty-seven do not.

And the four that vary track catalog scale, territory count and price-code count — structural facts any org can read off its own data — not selling motion.

A job does not need to know an org is Luxury Specification. It needs to know how many price codes it has. So: build the surface once, parameterise on the org’s own structure, and do not condition on segment.

This is the load-bearing product conclusion of the whole exercise, and it is the cheaper build. It is also far better supported than the alternative, because of what Layer A turned out to be — see the first finding below. Anyone proposing a per-segment build of an analytics surface is proposing to encode a distinction the data cannot carry. The default answer for any new job is does not differ by segment, and variation is claimed only where it can be pointed at a measured structural difference.

The correction to the premise: it is Sales Portal, not eCat Online

“All to do with eCat Online” does not hold, and the distinction matters more than it sounds. Per the platform anatomy, these are two separate surfaces with different actors: eCat Online is the buyer-facing surface with three modes — catalog, B2B cart, closed site — and its actor is manufacturer buyers and dealers. Sales Portal is the analytics and ops node, and its actor is sales leadership and CS. foundation/PLATFORM_ANATOMY_CURRENT_STATE.md §1.5–1.6, actor table

So the analytics layer lands overwhelmingly on Sales Portal (14 jobs), with eCat Online carrying five — all of them buyer jobs about buying, pricing entitlement and stock, not analytics. Conflating the two would have pointed the build at the wrong surface and the wrong audience.

Your iPad instinct was right, and better-founded than you knew

Two facts you did not have when you wrote that line:

  • There is no incumbent to displace. The Sales Portal Territory Dashboard — the surface a rep would use for exactly these jobs — requires :portal_portal and is enabled for zero organisations; the flag resolves to nine internal SuperCat usernames. Reports reaches 6 orgs. A shipped-but-disabled surface is not a shipped surface: anything rep-facing and analytics-shaped is greenfield, not a redesign, and design freedom is unusually high. product/surface-mapping.md §3
  • The embed is already precedented. The iPad codebase carries a WebView bridge at 36 call sites, classed NATIVE_EQUIVALENT; and Sales Portal is already embedded in the iPad’s WKWebView in the field, carrying an X-SuperCat-Request-Purpose: iPad Sales Portal header. Your “new little component thing” is an established pattern here, not a new architecture. product/surface-mapping.md §5 · PLATFORM_ANATOMY_CURRENT_STATE.md

The design constraints follow from the offline requirement rather than from taste. The component reads only from a pre-aggregated per-rep extract — roughly 6–8 MB, budget 15 MB for the large-catalog tail — never the raw tables. It is read-only by design, which removes write-conflict handling entirely. It must never block on a network call: a rep-facing panel that spins on a market floor breaks the one thing that already works and is our strongest asset. Permission scope is baked into the extract at sync time, and it fails closed — no valid extract, or an empty territory set, shows nothing, never whole-org.

FindingsWhat we now know that we didn’t

1. Our segments are stamped judgment corroborated by Postgres, not computed from it

We have been describing the v4.0 selling-motion segments as derived from first-party data. They are not. An authored rule over the four defining fields — average order value, price-code count, customer count, order volume — reproduces the stamped label 38.5% of the time against a 34.9% majority-class baseline (n = 109). Thresholds fitted directly to the answer key cap at 50.0%. The largest segment, SEG-02, has 21% recall.

The stamped v4.0 document says as much itself: it used the v3.2 validated segments as the null hypothesis, and states plainly that price “does NOT define this segment.” Postgres was run to test the v3.2 judgment, and it confirmed it; it did not generate it.

This does not weaken the segmentation. Stamped human judgment about observed selling behaviour is legitimate under our own epistemic standard. What was wrong is the lineage claim, which had been carried in 02_who_we_serve.md and CEO_SYSTEM_CONTEXT.md — both of which were telling every agent that the segments were data-derived and knowable at first touch. Both were corrected on 2026-08-26 and their prior versions archived. The operating rule that follows: the authority is a roster lookup, not a recomputation. The four fields are correlates and cannot be used to audit or overturn a stamped label, and a new quarter of order data does not re-segment anyone. taxonomy/account-segments.md §0 · FOUNDATION-CORRECTIONS.md

2. Prospects cannot carry a segment label — stated precisely

Everything that discriminates selling motion only exists after a company is a customer. We then tested whether public data could substitute, and the honest claim is narrower than the slogan:

Binary public feature extraction cannot predict a segment: 33.3% against a 34.5% majority baseline (n = 87). Holistic site reading gets to roughly 53%. Neither is good enough to label a prospect.

That is not “public data cannot predict segment.” The gap between the two numbers is method, not data — same web, different reader.

The worked case is Interlude Home: unmistakably a trade brand, scored TRADE_GATE = 0, because it gates by “DESIGNER RESOURCES” and “CREATE AN ACCOUNT” rather than “to the trade.” A person sees that in three seconds; a regex does not. This matters for what we do next, because the field method is the holistic one — a person with a browser forming a judgment, not a checklist. Overstating the finding would carry the wrong lesson into it.

The consequence is two explicitly separate layers: Account Segment (post-sale, roster lookup) and Prospect Archetype (pre-sale, named for what is observed, never inheriting a segment name). taxonomy/prospect-archetypes.md §5

3. Sales Portal’s real audience is buyers, not reps

7.7:1 — 17,532 active buyers against 2,274 active reps and internal users, on eCat Online portal orgs.

That ratio is measured on the portal surface itself. It is not a comparison against the 4,058 iPad-active reps, who are a different population on a different surface.

And those buyers are structurally confined. The analytics above Orders and Invoices are feature-gated: Reports reaches 6 orgs, Territory Dashboard 0, and the buyer’s own analytics page (:link_to_customer_dashboard) reaches 2 orgs. Roughly 79% of active buyers see a two-item portal — Orders and Invoices — and nothing else. That is the single largest unserved population in the product, and PER-08 carries six ranked jobs, more than any internal persona. personas/PER-08-dealer-buyer.md

4. Territory master coverage caps the first rep component at 16% of reps

The fail-closed territory rule is non-negotiable — an empty or zero territory set must show assigned-only or nothing, never a fall back to whole-org, which is the documented root trust failure and the fastest way to lose a rep permanently. That rule, combined with how few orgs actually maintain a territory master, caps who the first iPad component can reach:

The component launches to roughly 667 of 4,058 reps — 16% of the active base.

That is not an argument against building it: it is greenfield, nothing is displaced, and the extract and aggregation logic are reusable if the surface choice turns out wrong. But a build assumed to reach 4,058 reps that reaches 667 is the kind of surprise that kills a roadmap.

The implication is larger than the component. Fixing territory-master coverage unblocks more rep value than the build itself does — and it is a client data motion, a conversation with accounts about maintaining their own territory file, not engineering work. It is the constraint that most changes what to do next. engineering/02-IPAD-ACCOUNT-BRIEF-SPEC.md §2, measured by SQL 2026-08-27; data-gaps.md §B2 records this as “32 of 55 orgs” against a narrower roster, and the discrepancy resolves in favour of the query above because it is measured against exactly the population this component serves

That asymmetry argues for settling it before committing. The cheapest way to settle it is asking reps at market.

LimitsWhat this work does not establish

The jobs have not been validated in conversation with a single rep or buyer. The register is built from platform evidence — Postgres populations, feature-gate states, code paths, the Sales Portal spec and the platform anatomy — and from prior persona work. The jobs are grounded in observed product behaviour, not in interviews. That is the M1 Phase 2 gap, it is recorded as still-outstanding in the runtime context, and it is the natural next piece of work.

Two related limits, for completeness. Because the rep analytics surface is enabled for zero organisations, there is no usage evidence at all for it — no telemetry, no complaints from a surface nobody has, no baseline for what reps do today; every design assumption in Directive 3 is untested. And PER-05 product/merchandising is kept on the weakest evidence of the seven: it is inferred from Admin Console function clusters and catalog structure, not from a keyed user population.
