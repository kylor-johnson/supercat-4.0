Who uses each surface, what they care about, and what we should put in front of them

4,058 reps sell on the iPad, 87,927 dealer buyers are enabled on eCat Online with 21,260 active, and roughly 2,274 sales leaders, ops and customer-service people work in the Sales Portal — three near-separate populations that share almost no users. Seven personas across them have thirty-one jobs, each with one number that decides whether the job is done. We build one analytics layer, parameterised on the size and shape of each client's own data, and place each piece on the surface where the person already works — which for reps means a web component inside the iPad, not a portal they don't open.

The Admin Console holds the action half of several of these jobs — fixing a permission, correcting a product record, re-running an import. It is named inside the persona blocks where it does that work, but it is not treated here as a product to build analytics on.

Surface 1eCat iPad — offline, in the field

The install-base centre of gravity, and the only surface designed to work with no connectivity. Reps carry the whole catalog, their customer list, their pricing and inventory locally and write orders on it — in a dealer's back office, a designer's studio, a market showroom.

  • Independent sales rep PER-014,058 active · 146 orgs
  • Rep agency principal PER-02617 active in flagged rep groups
  • Internal admins on the iPad PER-03377 active

The independent sales rep — 4,058 of them

What matters to them in the job
They carry several manufacturers' lines and they are paid on what ships, not on what they write. Their book is a territory, not the company. The thing that decides how an appointment goes is walking in already knowing where the account stands — what it bought, what's still open, and what it has quietly stopped buying — instead of asking the dealer and looking unprepared. Their documented failure with us is trust: they pick a territory, still see the whole company's numbers, stop believing any of it, and rebuild the answer in Excel.
What we surface, and where
An account panel inside the iPad, opened from the customer they already have selected — no separate app, no login of its own:
  • Invoiced net for this account, last twelve months against the prior twelve, with open order value, last order date, and the categories they bought last year and haven't this year JTBD-011
  • Which of my accounts have gone quiet — accounts down more than a set percentage over ninety days, computed against that account's own history rather than a flat rule JTBD-014
  • Where is my order — open orders with status and what we can defensibly say about dates JTBD-015
  • Only my territory, and it adds up — the displayed total matches the book they were assigned JTBD-012
On quota specifically. You framed the rep example as quota. We cannot build that: quota data exists in exactly one client org, commitment reports in one, placement reports in three. A quota feature would silently apply to almost nobody. What we can give every rep instead is the account-level truth above — and, where the invoice feed is missing, a labelled order-based version of it. Insightful 4.0 provenance_map_rep.md §3.4
Today versus new
Selling offline on the iPad already works and is our strongest asset — that one is protect, don't touch. Everything above it is new. Territory scoping exists but is broken today and is a fix rather than a build. The underlying calculations are not speculative: account decline with dollars at risk, order-versus-invoice discipline, and rep activity and coverage all already run in our reporting factory — what is missing is a place to put them in front of a rep.
What blocks it
Territory coverage caps who can see it at all — see the component section below — and the invoice feed decides whether the numbers are invoiced dollars or a labelled order-based proxy.

The rep agency principal — 617, and we can't serve them

What matters to them in the job
They run a firm that carries the line, with sub-reps underneath them. What they need is the agency's whole book with one manufacturer, which sub-rep is actually carrying it, and enough evidence to defend the agency's value at renewal.
What we surface, and where
Nothing, this cycle. There is no concept of an agency anywhere in our system — the only link from a person to their firm is a free-text company name typed 11,873 different ways. Anything built on that would merge two agencies that spelled their name differently and split one that didn't, and hand a principal a confident, wrong number in the meeting that decides their renewal. See what we're not building.

Surface 2eCat Online — catalog, cart, closed site

The buyer-facing surface, in three modes: browse the catalog, buy through the B2B cart, or an authenticated closed site. Same catalog spine the rep sees on the iPad. This is where our customers' customers actually are.

  • Dealer buyer PER-08 — our customer's customer87,927 enabled · 21,260 active

The dealer buyer — the largest population on any surface we run

What matters to them in the job
They run a store or a design practice. They reorder what they know sells, they need to know they're seeing their price rather than a list price, and they will not promise a customer a delivery date on stock they can't verify. They are not our client's employee — they are our client's customer, and every job here is written from their side but justified by what our client gets from it: orders captured, calls deflected, share of wallet held. Anything good for the buyer and neutral for our client is deliberately left out, which is why "compare this manufacturer's prices to others" is not on the list.
What we surface, and where
In the catalog and cart they already use:
  • Their own price on every product they look at — the entitlement they're actually on, not list JTBD-083
  • Reorder what they bought before, without calling anyone JTBD-081
  • Stock, with its age visibly on it — availability is shown today with no indication of how old the snapshot is, which is how a buyer ends up promising something we can't ship JTBD-084
  • What they ordered and what they were billed, self-service JTBD-082
  • Their own account performance over time — the one genuinely new thing here, and a switch rather than a build JTBD-086
Today versus new
Pricing entitlement and order-and-invoice lookup already work — protect them, don't regress them. Cart is entitled in 58 orgs. Inventory freshness is a small build: the timestamp exists on every import and is simply never shown. The buyer's own purchase history is a configuration change, not a build — it reaches 2 orgs today, and turning it on across portal orgs would reach roughly 13,800 more active buyers. That is the largest reach available anywhere in this document for the least engineering.
What blocks it
Purchase history is blocked on a client-risk call, not on engineering — see decisions.
Where this persona spans surfaces
Buyers also account for most of the Sales Portal's live traffic. They are a distinct half of that surface and are handled there rather than duplicated here. They never cross into the rep views — a buyer account cannot reach them by any configuration.

And most of those buyers see almost nothing: roughly 79% of active buyers get a two-item portal — Orders and Invoices — and no more. It is the single largest unserved population in the product. PER-08

VP Sales and sales ops — one person, in most of our clients

What matters to them in the job
They own the selling system rather than a book. They answer upward for team performance, and the thing that ends their week badly is defending a number they can't source — reporting a total to the owner or a board and being unable to say where it came from. Their two documented frustrations with us are exactly that: the export doesn't match what the screen showed, and access rules are scattered across 134 toggles and six layers with no screen that shows who can see what.
What we surface, and where
In the Sales Portal:
  • One topline that reconciles — invoiced net for the period, company-wide, with prior-year and open backlog beside it, and a line saying what it's built from JTBD-031
  • The export and the screen agree. Same query both paths. This is a defect fix and it gates trust in every other number on the surface JTBD-035
  • Is the team actually using it — reps active in the last thirty days against seats licensed JTBD-032
  • Why can't this person see their book — one screen that answers a permission question instead of a support ticket; the fix itself happens in Admin JTBD-033
  • Did last night's data land — import failures surfaced in the morning rather than discovered at month end; also an Admin screen JTBD-034
Today versus new
The topline exists and needs honest labelling rather than rebuilding. Export reconciliation is a defect fix. Team usage is already computed and switched off — the rep-activity data exists and the flag is on for 7 of 257 orgs; turning it on is a config change. Rep activity, coverage of the assigned book, and quote-to-submit discipline all already run in our reporting factory and need no ERP feed to work, which means this persona can be served in full even at clients who send us no invoices. The permission and import screens are genuinely new.
What blocks it
Nothing structural. Three of the five are a config flip, a defect fix and an aggregation of data we already hold.

Customer service and order entry — named as a primary portal user, never counted

What matters to them in the job
The queue sets their day. They are measured on how fast and how accurately they answer, not on revenue. What they need is to reconstruct one account's whole story quickly when a dealer calls disputing something, and to key an order from a phone call or an email without getting the price level wrong.
What we surface, and where
Resolved on first contact is their number. The portal's customer-and-order history view is already the best existing fit in the product for this JTBD-041; the order-entry work itself happens in Admin. The one new thing worth building is catching the order that will fail before it fails — orders corrected downstream per thousand JTBD-044.
Today versus new
Mostly served already. This persona is better served by widening coverage of what exists than by new screens.
What blocks it
Order-failure reasons aren't recorded anywhere, so the failure metric can only be inferred from support tickets today. And we cannot size this population — there is no customer-service role in the system; they sit inside the 2,365 admin records, which is itself worth knowing.

Owner / exec — consumes conclusions, not screens

What matters to them in the job
They own the business, not the selling system. They log in rarely or never, and more often receive the number from someone who did. What matters is one true topline with its provenance attached, and early warning on accounts quietly slipping.
What we surface, and where
A thin portal subset — the topline and the risk list, nothing more — plus which accounts are quietly slipping, in dollars at risk JTBD-062. The deep version of this already exists and ships as a report rather than a screen, and that split is deliberate: an owner who logs in twice a year does not need a dashboard.
Today versus new
The topline is served. Account decline in the portal is new, and it needs a per-account baseline so that a lumpy project-driven account isn't flagged every time it finishes a job.
What blocks it
The invoice feed. This is one of the six jobs that cannot ship at all without it.

Product and merchandising — works in Admin, needs evidence from the Portal

What matters to them in the job
They own what's in the catalog and how it's presented, and they're judged on whether the assortment sells — not on any individual transaction. Their year runs to market, and the week before market is when a broken product record costs real money.
What we surface, and where
Where the catalog is broken — active items missing images, prices or taxonomy — which is the cheapest genuinely new job in the whole register, needs no new data, and lands in Admin where they'd fix it JTBD-053. Alongside it, what actually sold, ranked by units and invoiced dollars JTBD-051, and whether a new introduction landed JTBD-054.
What blocks it
"Did the new range land" needs a launch date on a product — one nullable column that doesn't exist yet. And this is the weakest-evidenced persona we kept: the work is demonstrably being done, but no role in the system identifies the person doing it. First to cut if customers don't confirm it.

The rep componentWeb, embedded in the offline iPad app

Your instinct here was right, and better supported than you knew when you wrote it.

Why embedded rather than native. The iPad already carries a web bridge at 36 call sites, and the Sales Portal is already embedded inside the iPad in the field today, carrying its own request header. This is an established pattern in our own codebase, not a new architecture.

Why the iPad rather than the Sales Portal — the direction, since you asked for one. The portal surface a rep would use for exactly these numbers is switched on for zero organisations; the flag resolves to nine internal SuperCat usernames, and the wider reports feature reaches six clients. Reps have never been served there. Meanwhile 4,058 of them are on the iPad every week, and the work happens where there is no connectivity — a dealer's back office, a market floor. Putting rep analytics in the portal builds a correct screen reps cannot open where they work. So: rep-facing analytics ship in the iPad component; the Sales Portal stays leadership, customer service and the buyer half. The one thing that would change my mind is reps themselves saying otherwise, and the cheapest place to ask them is market.

What it shows. One scrollable panel opened from the customer the rep already has selected: invoiced net for this account against last year, open orders, last order date, what they've stopped buying, and a quiet-account flag. It reads only from a pre-aggregated extract of roughly 6–8 MB that syncs with the catalog, never from live queries. It is read-only by design, which removes conflict handling entirely. It never blocks on the network — a panel that spins on a market floor is worse than no panel, because it breaks the one thing that already works. Every number carries its age on screen. If the territory scope is empty, it shows nothing rather than falling back to the whole company.

It launches to about 667 of 4,058 reps — 16% of the active base, in 20 client orgs.

Because the rule that it must never show a rep someone else's book means it can only run where the client actually maintains a territory file. Today 122 of 145 orgs with active reps have none. Of the 667 who qualify, 618 are also at clients who send us invoices, so they get real dollars rather than a labelled order-based stand-in.

That is not an argument against building it — nothing is being displaced, and the calculation work is reusable. It is an argument for fixing territory files alongside it, because that unlocks more rep value than the component does. It's a client conversation, not engineering.

What we are not building, and why

Per-segment versions of anything. We checked whether the analytics have to differ by the kind of client. Only 4 of 31 jobs vary at all, and the four that do vary on how many products, territories and price codes an org has — facts any client can read off their own data — not on how they sell. A job doesn't need to know a client is a luxury specification brand; it needs to know they have thirty-five price codes. So we build once and parameterise on the org's own shape. That is the cheaper build and the better-supported one.

The rep agency principal. Not servable until an agency exists as a real thing in the system, and the cost is normalising 11,873 typed-in company names, not the schema. There is no half version that isn't quietly wrong. Recommend saying no this cycle, out loud, rather than shipping an approximation.

Six jobs that need the invoice feed. Where the question is "what really happened commercially" — a defensible topline, the owner's true number, agency renewal value, where an order actually is — order data is not a substitute and we suppress rather than approximate. Seven other jobs degrade honestly to an order-based view and ship with a mandatory label. The feed is present at 38 of 109 clients.

Anything we can't compute. Commission, margin, cash and collections, customer satisfaction: real jobs, no data, and we don't estimate them. Commission is the notable one — a rep is paid on what ships, and the system they use every day can't tell them what they've earned. That's a documented gap with a known cost, not a shrug.

Decisions

1Confirm reps get their analytics on the iPad, not the Sales Portal. My recommendation above. Getting this wrong toward the iPad costs a wrapper — the calculations survive. Getting it wrong toward the portal costs the wrapper and leaves reps unserved where they actually work, and forces the iPad build anyway. Settle it before committing the build; asking reps at market is the cheap way.
2Turn on purchase history for dealer buyers? A configuration change reaching roughly 13,800 additional active buyers, not a build, so it competes with nothing for engineering time. It is held on a commercial question, not a technical one: showing a buyer their own history can expose what tier they're on and what they buy, and that is our client's call about their own customers. Two clients already run it and can be asked.
3Say no to the rep agency principal this cycle? Recommendation on file is yes — say it explicitly to anyone asking for agency analytics rather than shipping something that merges two agencies and looks authoritative.
4Run a client data push — invoice feeds and territory files? These are the two constraints that gate the most value and neither is engineering work. 71 of 109 clients send us no invoice feed, which degrades 13 of the 31 jobs. 122 of 145 orgs with active reps have no territory file, which caps the rep component at 16% of reps. Both are conversations with accounts. Together they unlock more than the entire build list.

AppendixMethod, and what this doesn't establish

None of the above depends on this section. It is here so the claims are auditable.

Our client segments are stamped judgment, not a computation

We have described the v4.0 selling-motion segments as derived from our own data. They are not. A rule built over the four defining fields — average order value, price-code count, customer count, order volume — reproduces the stamped label 38.5% of the time against a 34.9% majority-class baseline across 109 clients, and caps at 50.0% even when the thresholds are fitted to the answer key. The segments came from selling-motion judgment carried forward from v3.2; the data confirmed them, it did not produce them. This does not weaken them — stamped judgment about observed selling behaviour is legitimate — but it changes the operating rule: the authority is a roster lookup, not a recalculation, and a fresh quarter of orders does not re-segment anyone. Two foundation documents carried the wrong lineage claim and were corrected on 2026-08-26, with prior versions archived. taxonomy/account-segments.md §0

Why we don't put a segment label on a prospect

Everything that distinguishes selling motion only exists after a company is a customer. Testing whether public information could substitute: mechanical feature extraction from company websites reached 33.3% against a 34.5% baseline — no better than guessing. A person reading whole sites reaches roughly 53%. The precise claim is that mechanical extraction cannot predict a segment, holistic reading gets to about half, and neither is good enough to label a prospect — not the broader "public data can't predict segment," which overstates it. The worked case: a brand that plainly sells to the trade scored zero on the trade test because it says "designer resources" instead of "to the trade." A person sees that in three seconds. taxonomy/prospect-archetypes.md §5

Honest limits

  • No customer has been interviewed. The thirty-one jobs are built from platform evidence — populations, feature-gate states, code paths, the portal spec and the platform anatomy — and from prior persona work. They are grounded in observed behaviour, not in conversation with a rep or a buyer. That validation is the natural next piece of work.
  • No usage evidence exists for the rep surface. Because it has never been enabled for anyone, there is no telemetry, no complaint stream and no baseline for what reps do today. Every design assumption in the component section is untested.
  • Two personas cannot be counted. Customer service and product/merchandising have no role in the system that identifies them; their populations are UNKNOWN rather than estimated.
  • Insightful 4.0 is used here only as evidence of what we can compute — account decline with dollars at risk, price-realisation leakage, rep activity, coverage, quote-to-submit discipline all already run in that factory against live client data. It is a generated report, not a surface, and it is not proposed as one. The rep-facing operator built on it is early beta and nothing above depends on it.
